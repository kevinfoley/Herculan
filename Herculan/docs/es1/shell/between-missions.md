# GO.EXE — the career between missions

**Between two missions a career stands at the service bay, the hangar's picture with a strip of buttons along the bottom of the screen. Back from a mission, a talking head comments on how it went and the debriefing follows, then the bay; NEXT MISSION > there moves the career on to its next mission, through its talking heads to the war map. After a campaign's last mission whose outcome ends the career, an ending plays instead.** The career menu, its screens and the career's files are [the career's](career.md); this page holds the service bay's strip of buttons, what the shell does with a career mission's results, and how the career moves from one mission to the next.

Addresses are `ES1GO` image addresses ([the X-32 executable](../formats/x32-executable.md#the-image)). The functions here remove their own arguments, so argument lists are in stack order ([calling conventions](../formats/compiler.md#calling-conventions)). The roster is `Career_Roster` (`0009b218`) throughout ([the roster](career.md#the-roster)).

## Where the code lies

The service bay's handler `ServiceBayMenu_OnMessage` (`0002d327`) lies inside [the menu handlers' file](main-menu.md#where-the-object-files-start-and-end): it follows the last code of `CrewAssignMenu_OnMessage` (`0002d19e`), which ends at `0002d326`, and its `RET 8` at `0002d5bc` stands right before `Arming_DrawWeaponInfo` (`0002d5bf`). Ghidra's function for it holds only the dispatch and the shared exit; the case bodies from `0002d34d` are code its body leaves out. `es1_boilerplate.py bounds` over `0002d227`–`0002d6be` finds no object-file boundary evidence. The other functions here lie in [`career.cpp`](career.md#where-the-object-file-starts-and-ends), `0001601b` to `00016294`, and in code no batch has placed in its file yet: `HercStatus_OverallCondition` (`0001a323`) after `HercStatus_Ctor` (`0001a29c`); the HERC's, the stock's and the hangar's functions from `00022f05` to `0002a395`; and the repair costs from `00038b64` to `00038c50`, before `Shell_ProcessMissionResults` (`00038eb5`).

## The career's screen

**The career record's word `+4` names the screen the career stands at, the one RESUME, PROCEED and RESTORE open: 1700, the service bay before a mission; 1710, the service bay after one; or 1500, the war map.** `CareerRecord_Init` sets 1700 (`0x6a4`) ([the career record](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#the-career-record)); the bay's BRIEFING and NEXT MISSION > and `AnyDisabledAlert_OnMessage` (`0002a581`) set 1700, `Shell_ProcessMissionResults` 1710 after each mission ([after a mission](#after-a-mission)), and the war map's CANCEL 1500 ([CANCEL](war-map.md#cancel)). On any screen but 1710 those three load the career's next mission with `Career_StartMission` and build its map with `Shell_PrepareMission`; on 1710 they load none, NEXT MISSION > loading it ([RESUME](main-menu.md#resume), [PROCEED](career.md#proceed), [RESTORE](career.md#restore)).

## The service bay's bar

**Between missions the career shows the service bay, the hangar's picture with a strip of five buttons along the bottom of the screen. Before a mission the strip reads MAIN MENU, ARMING, CREW, BRIEFING and BEGIN MISSION >; after one it reads MAIN MENU, CAREER, REPAIR, BUILD and NEXT MISSION >, which checks that no HERC of the squad is disabled and moves the career to its next mission.** Screen 1700 is the bay before a mission, with the career's next mission loaded; 1710 is the bay after one, before the next is loaded ([the career's screen](#the-careers-screen)). Both are `ServiceBayMenu` (`00081fc6`) on backdrop 4000, with the handler `ServiceBayMenu_OnMessage` (`0002d327`) ([the screens](menus.md#the-screens)). The bay's own display and the screens its buttons open, ARMING (1800), CREW (1610), REPAIR (1900) and BUILD (2000), are the hangar's ([Open](#open)).

**Opening the bay.** Screen 1700's and 1710's case of `Shell_HandleMessage` (`0003efcc`) makes a `ServiceBayMenu` the current menu, sets the dword `0007fce4` to 0, which screen 2000's case sets ([the screens](menus.md#the-screens)), and, when `Shell_CurrentScreen`, still the screen being left, is 1610, CREW, loads backdrop 4000's palette again with `ShellBackdrop_LoadPalette`(`Shell_Backdrop`, 4000): 1610 is in group 4000, so the change of screen shows no new backdrop, and its case loaded `res32\regn1610.dpl`. In `Shell_Mode` 1, `Menu_Open`'s case for 1700 reads the byte `+1` of the record the roster's word `+7` names, which the war map's SERVICE BAY sets ([BRIEFING](#briefing)): while it is 0, BEGIN MISSION > reads BEGIN MISSION (`eng\watext.bin` line `0xc0`, through the button's `MTButton_SetTextFromTable`) and is made inactive (`+0x19` 2, `+0x1f` 1), and BRIEFING reads BRIEFING > (`0xc3`); otherwise they read BEGIN MISSION > (`0xc1`) and BRIEFING (`0xc2`). So a mission can be begun from the bay only after its briefing has been opened on the war map. Menu 1710 takes no such change.

`dlg\menu1700.dlg` and `dlg\menu1710.dlg` each lay out a plain window, style 1, id 1700, at (3, 183)–(318, 195) on the screen; five text buttons across it, MAIN MENU first with the key `Esc` and the last with `Space`, so the menu opens with the pointer on it ([opening a menu](menus.md#opening-a-menu--menu_open-0003969e)); and four kind-18 records, which add their rects, 1 to 3 pixels wide in the gaps between the buttons, to the full-screen render context's list ([the kinds](../formats/dlg-menu-dialogs.md#the-kinds)) (`HercWorks.Query menu 1700 1710`). `Menu_Close` destroys both windows with `DUIWindow_Dtor` ([closing a menu](menus.md#closing-a-menu--menu_close-0003b138)).

### BRIEFING

**BRIEFING plays the mission's talking head and then opens the war map, or opens the war map at once when the bay was opened from it.** It sets the career's screen `+4` to 1700 and saves the career with `Career_Save`(roster, the roster's dword `+7`). ## After a mission

**Back from a career mission, a talking head comments on how it went and the debriefing follows; its PROCEED > leads to the service bay, by way of a TECH RELEASE window when the mission made a HERC or weapon available. Before they show, the shell has taken the mission's salvage, recovered weapons, kills and the pilots' condition into the career, shared the salvage among the pilots, replaced each squadmate lost with a new pilot and scrapped the lost pilot's HERC, resupplied the weapon stock, made new equipment available, moved the hangar's marked HERC on, repaired HERCs out of each pilot's share and saved the career. With the player lost, or after a campaign's last mission whose outcome ends the career, an ending plays instead and the career menu opens.**

### Reading the results — `Shell_ProcessMissionResults` (`00038eb5`)

`Shell_ProcessMissionResults` reads the simulator's results back, in both modes:

1. In mode 1 it calls `Career_LoadCurrent` (`00016c1e`) with the roster, otherwise `SingleMission_RestoreRecord` (`00014fd6`), which reads `car\sm_play.gam` back into `SingleMission_Record` and loads `car\sm_play.mec` into the hangar ([the squad](../formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-squad)).
2. It opens `data\results.dat` (entry 7 of the file-name table, severity-5 assert at `shell.cpp` line 199) and reads a word into `000aad58`, the debriefing's ordinal, a dword into `0007fcf4`, the mission's salvage, a word count into `000817cc`, and that many pairs of words (*a*, *b*); in mode 1 `Inventory_Add` (`00028311`) pushes each *b* onto slot *a* of the weapon stock `000aad62` ([the weapon stock](../formats/car-hercs-and-stock.md#the-weapon-stock)), the shape in which `Herc_ScrapToSalvage` pushes a mount's condition onto its weapon's slot ([lost squadmates](#lost-squadmates)). It then reads the pilots' results with `CareerRecord_ReadPilotResults` (`0001568d`): it zeroes the words `0009ae5c`, `0009ae5e` and `0009ae60`, and for each entry of `CareerRecord_Current`'s record on strength with a slot, in entry order, it reads the pilot's condition `+0xf` and 6 bytes of gains (`PilotEntry_ReadGains`, `000153d8`, which applies them). For the player's entry 0, and for every entry in mode 1, `Hangar_ReadStatusBlock` (`00028e50`) reads 66 bytes over the status block of the HERC in the entry's slot, rebuilding its diagram, and the condition is that HERC's internal word 9, `Herc_GetStatus` (`00024b86`)(*h*, 9, `0x784`); when the slot holds no HERC nothing is read for the block and the condition is 0. The HERC read tests the literal index 0, not the record's `+0xe`. For a single mission's squadmates, entries 1 to 3 in mode 0, the 66 bytes go into a block of the function's own, built on the stack with `HercStatus_Ctor` and then read whole over, no HERC changes, and the condition is that block's internal word 9 ([the HERC record](../formats/car-hercs-and-stock.md#the-herc-record)). So each entry takes the 72 bytes the simulator writes for each machine of the player's group ([writing the results](../simulation/world.md#writing-the-results--mission_writeresults-0001d698)): 26 bytes of external words, 20 of internal words, of which bytes `0x2c`–`0x2d` are internal word 9, the condition, 20 of per-mount words, and the 6 bytes of gains; an entry read through a slot holding no HERC takes only the 6 bytes of gains.
3. It reads `data\mission.var` (entry 5, assert at line `0xd7`) through a `FileRWStream`, 200 bytes into the 100 flags at `000ab244`, closes it, and then sets flag 0 to `000aad58`.
4. In mode 1, on the record `0009b23b` + `0x1f7` × [`0009b21f`], it calls `Shell_StockItemsFromFlags` (`00038e48`), which for each *k* from 0 to 6 pushes a 100 onto the stock's slot of the weapon `Shell_FlagStockItems` (`00081834`)[*k*], 13, 2, 8, 11, 12, 4 and 3, once per count in flag 8 + *k* and then sets the flag to 0; adds the mission's salvage `0007fcf4` to `Career_Salvage` (`0007fcf0`), resupplies the stock with `Inventory_ResupplyWeapons` (`00028375`)(the stock `000aad62` points to) ([resupply](#resupply)), calls `CareerRecord_FinishMission` (`000160cb`) ([lost squadmates](#lost-squadmates)) and `Tech_Unlock`(record + `0xa8`, the record's word `+0xa`), the missions played as `CareerRecord_FinishMission` has just raised them ([equipment availability](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#equipment-availability)), moves the marked HERC on with `Hangar_AdvanceMarkedHerc` (`00029e01`) ([the marked HERC](#the-marked-herc)), and sets the career's screen `+4` to 1710. Otherwise it counts the single mission's pilots whose condition is 0 into the word `0007ebca` (`CareerRecord_CountLostPilots`, `00015db7`), which the debriefing shows as SQUAD LOSSES:, beside the totals `0009ae5c`–`0009ae60` ([the results panel](debriefing.md#the-results-panel)).

`Tech_Unlock` stores the kind and index of the last item it makes available at the record's `+0xa8` and `+0xaa`, which `CareerRecord_Init` sets to −1 ([equipment availability](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#equipment-availability)); the debriefing's PROCEED > opens screen 5555, TECH RELEASE, while `+0xa8` is not −1, and its PROCEED sets both back to −1 ([the debriefing's buttons](debriefing.md#the-buttons-and-keys), [RESUME](main-menu.md#resume)). Screen 5555's case (`0003d3b4`) only makes the `TechReleaseAlert` the current menu. `dlg\menu5555.dlg` lays out a menu window titled TECH RELEASE, id 999, at (49, 45)–(270, 152), PROCEED (100, `Space`) and three labels whose texts the build takes from the record ([the build](menus.md#opening-a-menu--menu_open-0003969e)): 5556 and 5557 the lines `+0xa8` + 2 and `+0xa8` + 4 of `eng\menu5555.bin`, 5558 the item's name. So a HERC reads "The tech boys have come up with", "a new herc:" and the chassis' name, a weapon "The tech boys have come up with a", "new weapon:" and the weapon's name (`HercWorks.Query menu 5555`). PROCEED posts 1710 ([RESUME](main-menu.md#resume)).

### Back at the shell

### Resupply

`Inventory_ResupplyWeapons` (`00028375`)(stock) takes the weapon numbers of `Inventory_ResupplyList` (`00080874`: 2, 8, 4, 12, 3, 1, 7, 11, 10, 13, 9, 6 and 0, ended by −1) in that order and pushes a 100 with `Inventory_Add` onto the stock's slot of each one that the current career record makes available, `Tech_IsAvailable` (`00028409`)(record + `0xa8`, the number, 1), and whose slot holds fewer than 2 (`Inventory_SlotCount`, `000282eb`), stopping after five pushes. It runs before `Tech_Unlock`, so a weapon the mission made available is first resupplied after the next mission; weapons 5 and 14 to 16 are not in the list ([the retail files](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#the-retail-files)). `Tech_IsAvailable`(state, index, kind) answers the available byte of the chassis entry *index* for kind 0, of the weapon entry for kind 1, and the kind itself for any other ([equipment availability](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#equipment-availability)); `Inventory_SlotCount`(stock, slot) answers the count word of the slot's stack ([the weapon stock](../formats/car-hercs-and-stock.md#the-weapon-stock)).

### Lost squadmates

`CareerRecord_FinishMission` divides `Career_Salvage`, the whole pool with the mission's salvage added, by *n*, the count `CareerRecord_CountPilots` returns for the record, unsigned and with no test of *n*, and *n* times steps to the next pilot (`CareerRecord_NextPilot`) and stores the share at its dword `+0x21`; it takes no share from the pool, which only the repairs draw on ([repairs](#repairs)). It then replaces the lost pilots and adds 1 to the missions played, the word `+0xa`. `CareerRecord_ReplaceLostPilots` (`00015e0b`) takes each entry *i* on strength with a slot and a condition of 0, adds 1 to `0007ebca`, the player's own entry counted too, and, when it is not the player's: with *k* its word `+4` and *v* its word `+6`, scraps the HERC in its slot with `Hangar_ScrapSlot` (`0002a20d`), sets the entry up again with `PilotEntry_InitRandom`, takes its name group from the word `0009b223` + 8(*i* − 1) + 2*k* of the roster, its name `+2` and `+6` from *v*, `+4` from (*k* + 1) mod 3, and, when that is 0, `+6` from (*v* + 1) mod 3. `PilotEntry_InitRandom` sets the slot `+8` to −1, so the new pilot has no HERC ([pilot entries](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#pilot-entries)). For an entry 0 that is not the player's, the name group's word `0009b223` − 8 + 2*k* lies before the roster's name groups.

`Hangar_ScrapSlot`(slot), for a slot that is not −1 and holds a HERC, scraps the HERC with `Herc_ScrapToSalvage` (`00024cec`)(HERC, 0), destroys it with `Herc_Dtor` (`000232a4`)(HERC, 3), clears the slot and takes 1 from the HERC count `Hangar_HercCount` (`00080892`). `000232a4`(HERC, flags), the HERC's destructor, does nothing for no HERC; otherwise it frees the diagram with `Herc_FreeDiagram` ([the mount pictures](../formats/herc-diagrams.md#the-mount-pictures)), destroys the member at `+0x72` with `Sprite_Dtor` (`00021519`)(HERC + `0x72`, 2) and frees the HERC with `Mem_Delete` when bit 0 of *flags* is set.

`Herc_ScrapToSalvage`(HERC, value), for each mount whose weapon is not 17, the empty mount: with the mount's condition (`Herc_GetStatus`(*h*, mount, `0x786`)) below 80, adds the weapon's value `000ab332`[weapon] × the condition / 100, the signed value word multiplied as an `int`, to `Career_Salvage`; otherwise pushes the condition onto the weapon's slot of the stock `000aad62` points to with `Inventory_Add`. Then it adds *value* to `Career_Salvage`, or, when *value* is 0, `Herc_FrameValue` (`00038d6c`)(HERC), the HERC's frame at its condition: the sum over the six external areas *g* of the chassis row's word *g* × `Herc_GetStatus`(*h*, *g*, `0x785`) / 100 and over internal words 0 to 8 of the row's internal word × `Herc_GetStatus`(*h*, *i*, `0x784`) / 100, each term truncated, its mounts not counted. A mount's weapon is `Herc_MountWeapon` (`00024ab7`)(HERC, mount), which sets the HERC's current mount `+0x70` to the mount, takes `Herc_CurrentMountWeapon` (`00024a6f`)(HERC), the word `+4` + 2 × `+0x70`, or 17 when `+0x70` is −1 or the mount count `+2` is 0, and puts `+0x70` back. `es2_xref.py` finds calls to `Herc_ScrapToSalvage` from `Hangar_ScrapSelectedHerc` (`0002a046`) and `Hangar_ScrapSlot`. The values are `damage.dat`'s ([the load](../formats/herc-catalogs.md#the-load)).

`Hangar_ScrapSelectedHerc` (`0002a046`)(), which `es2_xref.py` finds called from `ScrapAlert`'s handler `ScrapAlert_OnMessage` (`0002f111`) (screen 4444, [the screens](menus.md#the-screens)) and from `FabricationMenu_OnMessage` (`000309d7`), scraps the HERC in the hangar's selected slot `Hangar_SelectedSlot` (`00080894`) when it holds one. The marked HERC ([the marked HERC](#the-marked-herc)) is scrapped with the value `Hangar_MarkedValue` (`0008089c`) × 1000, then `Hangar_LeaveSelectedHerc` (`000295e7`), the destructor with 3, the slot cleared, `Hangar_MarkedMissionsLeft` (`00080898`) and `0008089c` set to 0, 1 taken from the count and `HangarBay_AssignSlotScripts` (`000120f9`)(slot, 1); the marked slot `Hangar_MarkedSlot` (`00080896`) keeps its number, so `Hangar_HasMarkedHerc` answers 0 for the empty slot. Any other HERC is scrapped with the value 0, so its frame's, then `000295e7`, the slot `+8` of the pilot entry `CareerRecord_FindPilotBySlot` finds for it in `CareerRecord_Current`'s record set to −1, the destructor with 3, the slot cleared, 1 taken from the count and `Hangar_NextHercSlot` (`00029729`). Both then call `Screen_BlindsClear` (`0004c053`)(8, 0, 1) and `Hangar_ShowSelectedHerc` (`000294d8`) ([Open](#open)). Screen 4444's text "This Herc will yield ### tons of salvage." takes its number from `Hangar_FormatScrapValue` (`0002a1aa`)(text), which formats `Herc_FrameValue` (`00038d6c`) of the selected slot's HERC, divided unsigned by 1000, with `%3d` (`00080940`) and copies its first three characters over the `#` that `strchr` finds and the two after it ([the kinds](../formats/dlg-menu-dialogs.md#the-kinds)); for the marked HERC that is its frame's value, not the `0008089c` × 1000 its scrap adds. ES2 scraps a lost squadmate's machine in its debrief ([scrapping](../../retail/shell/armory.md#scrapping)); `es2_naming.py match` pairs no ES2 function with `Herc_ScrapToSalvage`, the best scoring 0.18.

### The marked HERC

`Hangar_AdvanceMarkedHerc` (`00029e01`)() does nothing unless `Hangar_HasMarkedHerc` answers 1. When the marked slot's word `Hangar_MarkedMissionsLeft` (`00080898`) is above 0 it takes 1 from it, builds the HERC's diagram again with the lowered word unless `Shell_LowMemory` is set ([the mount pictures](../formats/herc-diagrams.md#the-mount-pictures)), and sets the dword `Hangar_MarkedValue` (`0008089c`) to (*v* × 80 × *p* / 10000 + (100 − *p*) × *v*) / 100, *v* being the price word of `Chassis_PriceTable` (`000808b8`) for the HERC's chassis word `+0`, signed ([the load](../formats/herc-catalogs.md#the-load)), and *p* `Hangar_MarkedHercProgress` (`00029f93`)(): (*c* − the word) × 100 / *c*, *c* the chassis' word of `Chassis_BuildMissions` (`000808d8`), 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3 and 3 for chassis 0 to 7. When the word is then below 1, as it is at once for a word already 0 or less, it sets every status word of the HERC to 100 with `Herc_SetAllStatus`, builds its diagram again unless `Shell_LowMemory` is set, and sets the marked slot to −1 and `0008089c` to 0. So a marked HERC comes out at full condition after its chassis' count of career missions.

**The marked HERC is the HERC BUILD starts.** Screen 2000's entry gives the marked slot a free slot when it is −1 and selects it (`Hangar_SelectMarkedSlot`, `0002980a`), and BUILD makes a HERC of the shown chassis there with `Hangar_BuildIntoSelected` (`00029d68`), setting `Hangar_MarkedMissionsLeft` (`00080898`) to the chassis' word of `Chassis_BuildMissions` (`000808d8`) and `Hangar_MarkedValue` (`0008089c`) to its price word, and taking the price × 1000 from `Career_Salvage` ([BUILD](repair-and-build.md#build--screen-2000)). The dword `0008089c` is the HERC's scrap value in tons: BUILD's SCRAP and `Hangar_ScrapSelectedHerc` give it back × 1000, and BUILD's panel shows it as SALVAGE ALLOCATED. The other writers `es2_xref.py --decode` finds are `Hangar_Clear` and `Hangar_Read` ([the HERC lists](../formats/car-hercs-and-stock.md#the-herc-lists)), and, for the slot alone, `Hangar_ShowSlot` (`00029651`) ([showing a bay](hangar.md#showing-a-bay)).

### Repairs

**After each career mission the shell repairs the squad's HERCs on its own, each pilot paying for their HERC out of their share of the salvage, as far down a ladder of levels as the share reaches.** It is ES2's auto repair ([repair levels](../../retail/shell/armory.md#repair-levels)) with ES1's ladder and budget.

`CareerRecord_AutoRepairSquad` (`0001601b`)(record) picks the first pilot entry by the record's word `+0x1f5`: 0, entry 4, so none; 1, entry 1, the squadmates; 2, entry 0, all four; any other value leaves the entry as `EDI` held it. It calls `CareerRecord_AutoRepairFrom` (`0001605c`)(record, first), which for each entry from *first* to 3 on strength with a slot calls `Repair_Auto` (`00029bf3`)(the entry's share `+0x21`, its slot `+8`). The word `+0x1f5` is `Career_AutoRepairMode` (`0007fcf8`) as the career's saves store it ([saving](career.md#saving--career_save-000168b0)); `es2_xref.py --decode` finds it written by `Roster_ActivateRecord` and by screen 1910's handler `RepairPrefsAlert_OnMessage` (`0003052f`), `RepairPrefsAlert`'s, which stores only 0, 1 and 2 ([PREFERENCES](repair-and-build.md#preferences--screen-1910)). REPAIR's REPAIR ALL runs `Repair_Auto` on the selected HERC with the whole pool as its budget ([REPAIR ALL](repair-and-build.md#repair-all)). ES2's `Game_AutoRepairSquad` takes its mode from `prefs.cfg` and gives every machine the same budget.

`Repair_Auto`(budget, slot) does nothing for slot −1 or an empty slot. Otherwise, on the slot's HERC, it first takes the entry `CareerRecord_FindPilotBySlot` finds for the slot in `CareerRecord_Current`'s record, then walks ES2's ladder ([repair levels](../../retail/shell/armory.md#repair-levels)) with `Repair_HercCost` (`00038c50`)(the level's target, HERC): from level 0, one level down while the cost is above the budget, compared unsigned, and the level is below the HERC's own, `Repair_LevelForCondition` (`000389fb`) of `Herc_OverallCondition` (`00024b6a`), which is `HercStatus_OverallCondition` (`0001a323`)(the status block, the mount count `+2`). When the cost is then below the budget, compared unsigned, it sets every status word to the level's target with `Herc_SetAllStatus`, takes the cost from `Career_Salvage`, and stores budget − cost as the entry's share; a cost equal to the budget stops the walk and repairs nothing. For a slot no entry holds the entry is −1, and the share's store lands on the record's dword `+0x10`; from `CareerRecord_AutoRepairFrom` the slot is always an entry's. ES1's ladder has five levels where ES2's has six:

| Level | Condition (`RepairLevelTable`, `0008179c`) | Repaired to (`RepairTargetTable`, `00081790`) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 91–100 | 100 |
| 1 | 75–90 | 90 |
| 2 | 52–74 | 74 |
| 3 | 1–51 | 51 |
| 4 | 0 | 0 |

`Repair_LevelForCondition`(condition) answers the first of the five whose floor is at or below the condition, and 0 for a condition below 0. `Repair_HercCost`(target, HERC) sums `Repair_ItemCost` (`00038b64`)(target, condition, value) over the six conditions `Herc_GetStatus`(*h*, *i*, `0x785`) with the values of the chassis' row of `000a0580`, internal words 0 to 8 with the row's internals (`+0x1a`), and each mount whose weapon is not 17 and whose condition is not 0 with `000ab332`[weapon] ([the load](../formats/herc-catalogs.md#the-load)); unlike ES2's it does not test for a null HERC. `Repair_ItemCost` is ES2's: 0 when the condition is at or above *target*, otherwise (the target of `Repair_LevelForCondition`(*target*)'s level − the condition) × value / 100, which for each of the five targets is the target itself.

## Moving on

**NEXT MISSION > moves the career to the next position of its campaign, or, past the campaign's last position, to the campaign the last mission's outcome leads to. A campaign table entry of −1 ends the career lost and 99 ends it won: the shell plays the ending after that mission, and the career can be played on only from a backup.**

`Career_AdvanceCampaign` (`00015a5a`) moves a career record on with `BattleSet_Advance` (`000141d0`) and returns whether its campaign changed. `BattleSet_Advance` adds 1 to the position (`Campaign_NextPosition`, `0001431f`, which is true while the position is at least 0 and below the campaign's count); past the campaign's last position it sets the campaign to the entry of `BattleSet_NextCampaignTable` (`0007ead0`) for the campaign and the outcome, `Career_OutcomeChoice` ([a career's missions](../formats/bst-battle-sets.md#a-careers-missions)), and the position to 0. `Career_PeekNextCampaign` (`00015aa5`) returns, through `BattleSet_PeekNextCampaign` (`0001418e`), 0 when the next position is in the campaign, and otherwise the campaign the table gives, writing nothing. No entry of the table is 0.

| Campaign | Outcome 0, the mission won | Outcome 1, the mission lost |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 1 | 3 | 4 |
| 2 | 3 | −1 |
| 3 | 5 | 6 |
| 4 | 5 | −1 |
| 5 | 7 | −1 |
| 6 | 7 | −1 |
| 7 | 99 | −1 |

`es2_xref.py` finds one call to `Career_AdvanceCampaign`, from `Roster_AdvanceCareer`, which NEXT MISSION > and the disabled warning's PROCEED call; neither tests the campaign. It finds three calls to `Career_PeekNextCampaign`, each of which treats −1 and 99 as the career's end:

- `Shell_OpenFirstScreen` after a mission, which plays the won ending for 99 and the lost one for −1 and opens the home menu, the career menu ([the first screen](menus.md#the-first-screen--shell_openfirstscreen-0003ba47));
- RESUME and PROCEED, for either: screen 1260, `dlg\menu1260.dlg`'s window CAREER OVER with "Please restore a backup to replay this career." and DONE ([RESUME](main-menu.md#resume), [PROCEED](career.md#proceed)).

So the career at its end keeps its last position and the screen 1710, and RESTORE of a backup is the way on ([RESTORE](career.md#restore)).
