# Handoff — mission editing in the HERCULAN Editor

Feasibility and scope for three features in the editor, `Herculan.Engine.Editor` (run on its own by `Herculan.Engine.Host.Editor`, and from the game's Editor menu item):

1. Showing and selecting the mission's non-object features — trigger areas, routes, groups, objectives and the action wiring between them.
2. Moving a herc to a new starting position.
3. Saving the edited mission.

How the editor holds a mission as its `.MSN` and writes each edit, what building a mission from nothing still needs, the scope and the suggested sequencing are [`handoff-editor-authoring.md`](handoff-editor-authoring.md).

## Headline

None of this is a from-scratch build. `MissionLoader` resolves almost every mission feature the editor could show into `Mission`, and the editor already shows and selects trigger areas, routes, the mission box, groups, objectives, timers and the action wiring ([In place](#in-place)); what is left of the first feature is the same kind of drawing and UI work. The `script.dat` writer exists and is complete: `ScriptDatTransformer.Write` covers all 13 blocks, and `HercWorks.UI.MissionScriptForm` already drives it through an open/edit/Save As UI. The editor holds a VOL mission as its `.MSN` and `.ENG` (`EditorMission.Source`, a `MissionDocument`), with the `script.dat` the scene is loaded from generated from them, and moves roster objects, route waypoints and groups, changes groups' sides, adds structures, hercs, flyers and ground vehicles, deletes roster objects, creates, edits and deletes routes, creates, reshapes, moves and deletes trigger areas, creates, edits and deletes actions and timers, wires actions into groups, objects and orders, edits groups' orders, fits the player's squad, adds and removes squadmates, creates, edits and deletes objectives, edits the mission's objective, briefing and intelligence lines and sets the mission's theater and time of day by writing the `.MSN` and its `.ENG` and generating again ([In place](#in-place), "Moving objects, waypoints and groups", "Adding structures", "Adding hercs", "Adding flyers", "Adding ground vehicles", "Changing sides", "Deleting objects", "Routes", "Trigger areas", "Actions", "Timers", "Wiring", "Orders", "The player's squad", "Objectives", "The mission's text" and "The mission's theater and time of day"; [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)). A plain `script.dat` opens read-only. Save writes the `.MSN` and its text into the install, where the game loads them in place of the archive's ([In place](#in-place), "Saving"). File > New mission starts a mission on a chosen zone and theater, by day or night, the player's machine alone with one nav point to reach ([In place](#in-place), "New mission"); building one up from there needs the creation and field editing of every record kind ([Authoring a mission from scratch](handoff-editor-authoring.md#authoring-a-mission-from-scratch)).

Whether DBSIM can read a grown file is settled (it can — [gap 2](#2-three-consequences-of-appending)).

## What already exists

- `Mission` carries `Actions` (each `MissionAction` with its resolved `MissionTriggerArea` list), `ActionTimers`, `GroupOrders` (each order's `Route` resolved to points), `PlayerRoute`, `Objectives`, `GroupDeploymentActions`, `GroupKinds`, `GroupSides`, `GroupOutOfActionReports`, `Coordinates` and `Text`. Each `MissionPlacement` carries its `GroupIndex`, `SlotIndex`, `EngagementActionRef`, `DefeatActionRef`, `StartingCondition` and `OutOfActionReport`.
- `SceneItem.Visible`, for hiding waiting groups; the editor's own `GroundPick`.
- `MissionLoader.AddRoster` — `Coordinate(script, record.PositionRef) ?? OffsetFromGroup(...)`. The engine honours a roster record's own position override, matching `Mech_AttachToGroup` (`00417aa8`), and the editor's object moves write it.
- `MissionPlacement.Kind` + `SlotIndex` address a block-7/8/9 record exactly, and `GroupIndex` addresses block 11, so the editor's selection already identifies the record to edit.
- `HercWorks.UI.MissionScriptForm.OnSaveAs` — a working save path, with VOL entry-prefix preservation and an advisory cross-block ref-range validator.
- `MissionGenerator` (Core) builds a `script.dat` from an `.MSN` as VSHELL does, and `MissionFileTransformer` round-trips `.MSN` files.
- `HercWorks.Query actions|orders|objectives` — the corpus cross-check for anything the editor shows; `records <MISSION> [--row N]` and `header <MISSION>` dump one mission's raw `.MSN` records, and row 2 beside the header it generates.
- `MissionLoader.Load(content, script, path)` loads from a `ScriptDat` already parsed: `EditorMission.Open` hands it the one a `MissionDocument` generated, or a plain file's as read, and keeps it as `Document`; the mission check runs over that document as opened.
- Round-trip coverage: `TrainingLaunchTests.LoadsEveryRetailMission` writes a `script.dat` from each of the 62 `.MSN` files and checks `ScriptDatTransformer` rewrites it byte-exact, and `ScriptDatRoundTripTests` rewrites the install's `DATA\script.dat` and every `sav\script%d.dat` as a prefix of the file read (they keep stale tails past block 13). Both skip silently without an install.

## What a saved `script.dat` reaches

VSHELL writes `data\script.dat` afresh from the `.MSN` every time it loads a campaign or practice mission, and so does this engine's own campaign launch (`ShellCampaignLaunch`, through `MissionGenerator`). A `script.dat` exported from the editor's generated one is therefore played by: `Herculan.Engine.Host` opening that file directly; DBSIM run directly over `data\`; and a save slot, since `Career_LoadSlot` (`00412bbf`) copies `sav\script%d.dat` into `data\` and the replay plays it as-is ([`../retail/formats/save-games.md`](../retail/formats/save-games.md#the-slot-handoff)). It does not change a campaign mission, which is the `.MSN`'s ([The `.MSN` layer](handoff-editor-authoring.md#the-msn-layer)); a Save of the `.MSN` does ([In place](#in-place), "Saving").

## Writing the editor's UI

What the user has corrected in the editor's panels, which every new view follows:

- **Vocabulary.** The player's group is the squad, never a "lance"; its other members are squadmates, never "wingmen". The player *pilots* a HERC; "fly" is for flyers. The subject of a sentence is the player, the mission or the HERC, never "the game" ("the game flies X" says nothing). A group's side is its **Team**. A structure or ground vehicle is titled by its type and the name the MFD's F5 screen prints (`Base type 40: <name>`).
- **Plain words, short.** A note says what the user sees or can do, in one short sentence, not how the records produce it: "No heading of its own: it faces along its route's first leg", not "names none: the bearing of its route's first leg". Labels are the user's words ("Related objectives", not "Objectives asked of it").
- **No note for what a disabled control already shows.** A field the user cannot change is drawn disabled; a note is added only when the reason is not obvious, and a note saying only that something cannot be changed is left out.
- **Notes go through `EditorText`.** `EditorText.Note` draws every aside wrapped, in a grey lighter than ImGui's disabled text, which the 340-pixel Properties panel cuts off unwrapped and which is hard to read. `EditorText.Emphasis` draws, in the bold face, the one note per view the user must not miss (a campaign mission's squad set here is not used).
- **Latin-1 only in UI strings.** The ImGui font atlas holds Basic Latin and Latin-1, so an em dash or a curly quote draws as `?`. Use parentheses, colons and straight quotes; `°` is in range.
- **Identity first.** An object's view opens with its name and then its group, before any other field.
- **Errors point at the fix.** A refused input shows its reason in a strong red and tints the field to change (the New mission dialog's name).

## Mission features to show and select

Counts below are over the 62 retail `.MSN` files with campaign conditions not applied (`HercWorks.Query`).

### In place

- **Opening a mission.** The editor starts on `DATA\script.dat` or the file named on its command line, read-only ("A plain `script.dat`" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)), and with no mission and the VOL list up when there is none. File > Load from VOL lists every `.MSN` in the archives and every loose one in the install's `MSN\` folder, marking a loose one "(saved)" when it overrides an archive mission and "(new)" when no archive holds it, and opens one through `MissionOpener.Open` and `MissionFileLaunch.Open` (the simulator host's `.MSN` path), which writes the handoff into `%TEMP%\herculan-editor\`, so the install's `DATA\` is never written, and holds the mission as a `MissionDocument` whose generation draws what the handoff's load drew. For a mission loaded in training mode, a practice mission or one played as `INSTANT ACTION`, that generation writes nothing over the theater and the time of day, which keep what the mission's row 2 patches ([Mission level](handoff-editor-authoring.md#mission-level)); a campaign mission's is the launch's. The document's generated `script.dat` and `mission.str` are written over the handoff's, and the scene is loaded from them. `EditorMission` and `MissionDrawables` are rebuilt for it. Such a mission's `Mission.SourcePath` is that scratch file.
- **New mission.** File > New mission (`NewMissionDialog`) asks for a name, taken as Save As takes one but never an archive mission's, which the game would play as that mission in its career position, and asking first when the install has a saved mission of that name; a zone, from every `dat\ZONE*.DAT` with its `.DBA` in DBSIM's mount (`MissionZones`); a theater, each named by the terrain bank its day descriptor loads (`TheaterNames`: `urban`, `bsnow`, `volcan`, `ice`, `moon`, [`../retail/formats/wld-world.md`](../retail/formats/wld-world.md)), since no retail text names one; and day or night. `NewMission` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/NewMission.cs`) builds the `.MSN` and `.ENG`: the player's machine alone on a route of two points, with one mandatory objective met when the player reaches the second, every record copied from `TRAIN1.MSN` but the text package, which takes `TRAIN5.MSN`'s shape; its comments cite each record. `MissionOpener.OpenNew` and `MissionFileLaunch.OpenNew` open it from memory as `Open` opens a mission `gam\career.dat` does not list, as `INSTANT ACTION`, into the same scratch handoff, and the title carries `*` until File > Save writes it (`EditHistory.MarkNeverSaved`); the browser then lists it "(new)". The editor shows it on the theater and by the time of day the dialog chose, as for every mission that is not a campaign one ("Opening a mission"). The mission check finds nothing in it.
- **The mission's theater and time of day.** The outliner's first entry, Mission, shows the mission's view (`MissionView`): its name and zone, a Theater choice named as the New mission dialog names them, and Day or Night, then its text ("The mission's text"). A campaign mission's theater is its stage's and is shown disabled, with a note saying so; its day or night is its own and changes. `TheaterChanger` writes the header word, 0 for the theater and 9 (offset 18) for the time of day, in row 2 through `MissionDocument.SetHeaderWord` and generates again, as one step of the undo history, run once the frame's panels are drawn, and then dresses the scene in the world they pick, `wld\world<theater * 2 + variant>` (`MissionScene.ChangeTheater`): a model library in the new palette and shade ramp, every object's model and LOD chain built again from it, those of objects an edit took out and an undo can put back included, the terrain mesh lit again in the new bank, and the sky, fog, ground-shape set and beam colours. The terrain's heights and materials, the objects, the selection, the camera and the view settings stay, and `TerrainLeveller` keeps building rows of the terrain mesh against the scene's models as they stand. The host then frees every upload of the old world and uploads the scene again (`EditorHost.ReloadWorld`). A change costs about what opening a mission costs, without loading the zone. A HERC's shadow already on the ground keeps the radius its ground-shape set gave it; every retail descriptor names the same set (`FLAT2`). The mission's `Mission.Header` keeps the theater the mission was opened or last rebuilt with ("Rebuilding after an edit" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)); the view and the scene read the generated header.
- **Selection and panels.** `EditorSelection` is an object, group, trigger area, action, timer, route (with an optional waypoint) or objective, held in one `SelectionState` that the viewport click, the `MissionOutliner` (left: groups with members, areas, actions, timers, routes, objectives under the block-13 objective lines) and the `PropertiesPanel` (right, one view per kind, every mentioned record a link) all set.
- **Camera.** `EditorCamera` flies free (right mouse to look, WASD/arrows, Q/E, Shift). A double-click in the viewport or the outliner selects and frames the selection (`SelectionBounds`, `EditorCamera.Frame`), keeping the yaw and looking down on it. V, or View > Top-down, switches to a view looking down from above, north up, high enough over the middle of the zone that its whole grid fits the window; there looking turns about the vertical alone, W is up the screen, fog is off and the clip planes are pushed out with the camera's height. V again returns to the pose it left; opening another mission leaves it.
- **Picking.** A press on a move handle of the selection, the corners and radius of a selected trigger area included, is taken in screen space before anything is picked (`MoveGizmo`). Otherwise `ScenePicker` tries a waypoint in screen space, then an object (skipping waiting groups when they are hidden): the bounding spheres the ray meets give the candidates, and the nearest of their triangles it meets wins, a structure's ground plane counting only when it meets nothing solid, so a Herc on a base's pad is picked rather than the base; then `GroundPick` (a ray march against `HeightAtWorld`) and the smallest trigger area containing that ground point.
- **Overlays.** `MissionOverlay` draws, through `DrapedLines`, every trigger area of block 4 (coloured by who trips it, grey when no action tests it), every route of block 3 (the player's, the walked ones and the never-walked ones, those no order names among them, in three colours, loops closed, waypoints numbered when selected), every base pad's painted tile (`MissionBasePad.TileOrigin`, with the selected group's authored point and its snap drawn too) and the mission box with its HDD-map and abort margins, each toggled from the View menu. A line hidden behind terrain shows dimmed. A selected area's ground is tinted yellow over exactly what its test catches — a circle area's octagon included — through `SceneRenderer.Highlight`, compiled only into the editor's program as the grid is.
- **Cross-references.** `MissionIndex` cross-references, once, which actions test each area, which groups walk or merely name each route, and for each action the timers, engaged and defeated objects that fire it and the waiting groups, ended orders, started timers, counters and message (from `COMMAND<n>.STR`) that follow. The action view shows all of it, and an area's view shows it read-only for every action testing that area (`ActionView.DrawSummary`).
- **Groups.** `Mission.GroupSetups` carries each block-11 record's point (with its pre-snap authored point and where it came from), heading (and whether it is the route bearing), formation id and paints-ground flag. Selecting a group boxes its members, highlights the route it walks, and `GroupOverlay` draws a post at its point, a heading arrow, and a line to each order's subject labelled with the slot and verb. Its view adds the setup, the out-of-action counter writes and the objectives asked of it to the members, deployment action and orders ("Orders" below); an object's view adds its own counter writes and objectives.
- **Waiting groups.** View > Waiting Groups draws, outlines (meshes hidden, members boxed, still selectable) or hides the members of a group gated on an action. Unless hidden, each is labelled at its point with the action and the arrival (drop pod, on foot, in place), labels sharing a point stacked.
- **Mission check.** `ScriptDatLint` (Core) holds `MissionScriptForm`'s ref-range checks plus an action area ref behind its first `-1`, an action of type 7 to 10 with no target, an action with a message that writes no counter, which DBSIM never posts, an objective condition or subject kind with no case, a group with neither a heading nor a slot-0 order, a herc group with no slot-0 order, rosters past DBSIM's slot caps, and, given the line count, a `mission.str` past the 50 lines DBSIM reads. Both apps run it; the editor lists the findings under Problems, each selecting its record, and `HercWorks.Query lint` runs it over every retail mission's generated `script.dat`, finding one thing: `C1_02`'s action 1, the one action carrying a message and writing no counter, `MAYDAY!` ([`../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md`](../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md#its-speakerless-set)).
- **Moving objects, waypoints and groups.** Selecting a roster object (not one of the player's squad, which has no roster record) shows typed X and Y fields in its view, and Z for a flyer, the one height the spawn pass reads from the point ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule), rule 5); a field applies when it loses the focus. In the viewport `MoveGizmo` draws X and Y arrows, a square in the ground plane between them and, for a flyer or a flyer group, a Z arrow, on the selected object, waypoint or group, and on each corner of a selected trigger area ("Trigger areas"); dragging one previews the move and commits it on release. `PointMover` makes each edit: the commit writes the `.MSN` and generates `script.dat` again ("The tools" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)), and a drag's preview writes the generated `script.dat` alone. An object is given a point of its own: `MissionDocument.PlacePoint` rewrites its row-6 record in place when the object's field is all that names that GUID, in any record, and the GUID has one record, and appends a point with a new GUID otherwise, which the roster record that supplies the field then names; the sim object stands where the simulator would stand it. A waypoint moves the point it names, so whatever shares that point moves with it ([gap 1](#1-positions-are-indirect)); Alt+drag detaches it instead, through `MissionDocument.PlacePoint` as for an object, its route's list entry naming the point placed; a list a variant supplies is the alternative's, which every route drawing it shares. Either way its point is written with Z 0, and a closed route's first and last waypoints move together. A group moves the same way through the point it stands on, its block-11 record's own or else its route's first waypoint (rules 2 and 3), so the route's start and whatever shares the point move with it; Alt+drag gives the record a point of its own. Its handles sit on its authored point, which a paints-ground structure group's snap then puts in its tile, so its pad jumps a tile at a time; its members with no point of their own follow, the player's squad with group 0. Only a flyer group's Z is written from the drag, and any other keeps its point's Z; the group view says what else names the point. After every change to block 1, `PointFollowers` places again from the generated document whatever stands on a moved point ([gap 4](#4-live-scene-refresh)), `MissionDrawables.Move` and `ScenePicker.Move` following each object it moves, and the route view says how many other `.MSN` records name the selected waypoint's point. The mission box overlay is rebuilt from block 1. After every move, a drag's preview included, `TerrainLeveller` puts the heightmap and the cell materials back as the zone loaded them and runs the spawn pass's pad painting and footprint flattening again over every structure and base pad where they now stand, so the ground and a pad's paint follow as they move and return to the zone as loaded where they leave; structures and walking mechs are settled on the new ground, the changed rows of the terrain mesh are rebuilt and uploaded, and the overlay lines are laid again. Edit > Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) step through the edits, each putting back the `.MSN` rows it changed and generating again. The view says when the object is outside the mission box as opened, and when it is a group follower that holds formation on its leader once the mission runs.
- **Adding structures.** The Toolbox window (`EditorToolbox`) lists, under Structures, the 45 structure types of `BASES.DAT` under the names the MFD status screen prints for them, with a Human or Cybrid choice for the side. An entry dragged out and let go over the view adds that structure on the ground under the pointer; a double-click, or Add with an entry chosen, adds it on the ground at the middle of the view, or under the camera when that is sky, which the gamepad's pointer reaches as well. While an entry is dragged over the view, the structure it would add stands on the ground under the pointer (`DropPreview`, drawn but not picked, listed or levelled under). Letting go over a panel adds nothing. With a structure group or one of its structures selected, "Join the selected group" adds the structure to that group instead: its record names a point of its own where it was put, it takes the group's first member slot of the 20 that no record of the group's GUID fills in any state (DBSIM walks them by raw index, [`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule) rule 7) and the group's side, and names no heading, so it faces the group's way. Otherwise `StructurePlacer` appends to the `.MSN` a row-14 record naming no point, heading or action of its own, pristine and writing no counter, and a row-16 group of its own with it in slot 0, standing on a row-6 point appended at the ground's height and facing a row-7 heading of 0 degrees (a lone unconditioned row-7 record reading 0 is shared), in formation 0, not painting its ground, with no orders, no deployment action and no counter writes; each record is unconditioned, with a new GUID. That is the shape of 148 of the retail missions' 537 structure groups (`HercWorks.Query orders`), and the mission check passes it, since a group naming a heading needs no slot-0 order. An add that would take block 9 past DBSIM's 140 structure slots is refused: the Toolbox's structures and ground vehicles, which share block 9, are disabled, with the reason under each heading, while its hercs and flyers, gated by the caps of blocks 7 and 8, are not. The editor's state is then built again from the generated file ("Rebuilding after an edit" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)), which builds the object through `MissionScene.CreateObject`, the scene load's own path, its model built and uploaded then when nothing else in the mission draws with it; the ground is levelled under it as after a move, and it is selected. The add is one step of the undo history: Undo takes the records out again and rebuilds, and Redo puts the same ones back, under the same GUIDs, with the same object standing for them. An added structure and its group move as any other.
- **Adding hercs.** The Toolbox's Hercs list offers the 21 mech types of `nam\MECHS.NAM` under the names the MFD prints, added as a structure is (dragged onto the ground, double-clicked or Add) with the same Team choice; while one is dragged over the view it stands on the ground under the pointer at its ride height, a RAZOR 5,000 units higher, where it starts (`DropPreview`). `HercPlacer` adds it, through `HercGroups` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/HercGroups.cs`), in a new group: a row-6 point at the drop point (Z 0) and a row-8 route listing it (`RouteEditing`), a row-15 search/destroy order on that route whose subject is group 0, the player's squad, a row-12 herc naming no point or heading of its own, and a row-16 group with the herc in member slot 0 and the order in order slot 0, naming no point and no heading, so it stands on its route's only waypoint facing heading 0, its own route field naming the route; each unconditioned, with no variant key and a new GUID. That is the shape retail gives a herc group most often: of the 433 records of retail herc groups outside the squad that name a member, 288 have a slot-0 search/destroy on a group with a route (182 on the squad) and 402 name no point. The fields with no obvious value take the one most retail records of their kind carry, and `HercGroups`'s comments give each count. A Human chassis (types 0 to 8) comes with its stock fit from `gam\ini_*.dat`, as a chassis change in the squad gives it; a Cybrid (types 9 to 20), which no shell file fits and every mission fits in its own records, often through a variant drawn at random, with the fit the most retail records of its type carry, the first in mission and record order where two tie (the editor's choice). With a herc group or one of its hercs selected, "Join the selected group" adds the herc to that group in the first member slot no record of the group's GUID fills, up to slot 7, the last `dat\MFORMS.DAT` spreads (the editor's limit); its record names no point, so it stands in its formation place, and it takes the group's team. Group 0 is refused, with the reason in the Toolbox, since its view adds squadmates. An add past DBSIM's 100 block-7 slots, which the squad's `player.mec` machines share, counted as the mission check counts them, is refused: the Toolbox's hercs are disabled, with the reason under their heading. The add is one step of the undo history; the editor's state is then built again ("Rebuilding after an edit" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)), which builds the herc through `MissionScene.CreateObject` as the scene load builds one, its model, LOD chain and loadout, and stands it at its ride height, and the herc is selected. The mission check finds nothing in the new group. Deleting its herc deletes the group and its order ("Deleting objects"); the route stays, and can then be deleted.
- **Adding flyers.** The Toolbox's Flyers list offers the types of `nam\FLYERS.NAM` the install has a `dat\<type>.DAT` for, under the name the MFD prints (`SKIMMER` as LANDSKIMMER); `HOVTANK` and `DROPSHIP`, which have no data to fly or be shot with ([`../retail/simulation/ai-flyers.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-flyers.md)), are named in a note and not offered. A flyer is added as a herc is ("Adding hercs"), with the same Team choice: `FlyerPlacer` adds it through `FlyerGroups` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/FlyerGroups.cs`) in a new group of the same shape, built by the same code (`NewGroups`), a row-13 flyer naming no point or heading of its own in a row-16 group of flyers. The route's waypoint has Z 0, so DBSIM starts the flyer at 5,000 units ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule), rule 5), as every retail flyer group starts, and the group's Z handle changes it after ("Moving objects, waypoints and groups"); while one is dragged over the view it stands at that height over the ground under the pointer (`DropPreview`). The flyer's and the group's other fields take the value most of the 84 retail flyer records of a type and the 76 records of flyer groups naming a member carry, and the comments of `FlyerGroups` and `NewGroups` give each count. With a flyer group or one of its flyers selected, "Join the selected group" adds the flyer to that group in the first member slot no record of the group's GUID fills, up to slot 3, the last `dat\FFORMS.DAT` places beside the leader (the editor's limit; no retail flyer group has more than two members). An add past DBSIM's 50 block-8 slots is refused: the Toolbox's flyers are disabled, with the reason under their heading. The add is one step of the undo history and rebuilds as a herc's does, the flyer built through `MissionScene.CreateObject` and selected. The mission check finds nothing in the new group; deleting its flyer deletes the group and its order, and the group keeps its last order ("Orders").
- **Adding ground vehicles.** The Toolbox's Ground vehicles list, between Flyers and Structures, offers the 15 types of `BASES.DAT` that DBSIM builds as ground vehicles ([`../retail/simulation/structure-behaviour.md`](../retail/simulation/structure-behaviour.md#five-classes-one-switch)), under the names the MFD prints; the other five vehicle types, for which it builds no object, are named in a note and not offered. A vehicle is added as a herc is ("Adding hercs"), with the same Team choice, while one dragged over the view stands on the ground under the pointer as a structure does: `VehiclePlacer` adds it through `VehicleGroups` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/VehicleGroups.cs`) in a convoy built by the same code (`NewGroups`), a row-14 vehicle naming no point or heading of its own in a row-16 group of structures whose slot-0 order is travel (verb 5) naming no subject, the shape of the 12 retail groups whose every member is a ground vehicle. The group does not paint its ground. With one waypoint the convoy's leader has nowhere to drive ([`../retail/simulation/ground-vehicles.md`](../retail/simulation/ground-vehicles.md#the-ground-vehicle-tick--0046a5d0)), so it stands until waypoints are added to its route ("Routes"), and the Toolbox says so. The fields take the value most of the 108 retail vehicle records and the 12 convoy records carry, and the comments of `VehicleGroups` and `NewGroups` give each count. A structure group whose first member is a ground vehicle is a convoy; with one or one of its members selected, "Join the selected group" adds the vehicle in the first member slot no record of the group's GUID fills, up to slot 3, since the leader pick, `Group_FirstAbleMember` (`0046a4b8`), reaches only the first four members (the editor's limit), naming no point, so it takes its formation post, as 29 of the 30 retail convoy members past slot 0 do. The timer view (`TimerView`) offers the action that starts it, none meaning it counts down from mission start, as the view says; the delay in seconds, from 0 to 32767: `ActionTimer_SetDelay` (`004679c0`) sign-extends the stored word into its 32-bit countdown, so a larger one reads as negative and fires at once; and the actions it fires, up to ten, each a choice with Remove and a link, and Add after the last, kept filled from the first (DBSIM fires every set slot). Delete in the view, or the Delete key with a timer selected, removes every row-11 record of the GUID: nothing references a timer, as the generator resolves no field of any row into row 11 and no `script.dat` field indexes block 6. Each edit is one step of the undo history and builds the editor's state again, which keeps the timer selected by its row-11 GUID.
- **Wiring.** A group's view offers the action it waits on, none or any action, with a link and how it then arrives; the player's squad has none, as none of the 138 retail squad records sets one. An object's view, a herc's, flyer's, structure's or ground vehicle's outside the squad, offers the action it fires when engaged and the one when defeated, each none or any action, with a link, a flyer's engaged action included, which DBSIM reads though no retail flyer sets it; a squad machine's are not offered, as none of the 278 retail records the squads reference sets either, and DBSIM builds the machine from `player.mec`. An order slot offers the action that ends it ("Orders"). `ActionWiring` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/ActionWiring.cs`) and `GroupOrders.SetAction` write each through `MissionDocument.SetField`: in the record supplying it, an overlay record for a roster object whose variant supplies it, and an unset value in every record of the GUID; an order another slot runs is copied first. A field a variant supplies offers no None, since an overlay cannot carry the unset value. Each edit is one step of the undo history and builds the editor's state again.

### Gaps in what is in place

- **Waypoint height.** A moved waypoint's point is written with Z 0, as 1487 of the 1488 retail waypoints have it. DBSIM reads a waypoint's Z as the starting altitude of a flight standing on its route's first waypoint, and the heads-down map projects the player's route icons with it ([`../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md#the-route-cursor-is-loaded-once)); everything else works in the ground plane. A waypoint has no field or handle for it; only a flyer group standing on its route's first waypoint sets it, through the group's Z handle. This engine's heads-down map places route icons by X and Y alone.
- **Waypoint and group views.** The route view lists its waypoints' coordinates and the group view its point read-only, with no typed fields as an object's view has, and neither they nor the area view warn when a waypoint, a group or an area's point is moved outside the mission box as opened, though that grows the box and the heads-down map's frame as an object does (the box overlay shows it).
- **Trigger areas.** A shape change and a radius edit write the area's supplying record alone: another record of its GUID, conditioned, keeps its own shape and points in the states it survives in. An area has no field for a height, which DBSIM's test ignores. A selected area's handles sit on every corner at once; two corners closer on screen than the handles' reach are taken in the order the box names them.
- **Area tint.** Only a selected area is tinted. A selected action's areas are outlined, not tinted: `SceneRenderer.Highlight` holds one region. The tint is painted on the terrain alone, not on a structure's ground plane (its shadow and floor plates) inside the area.
- **Adding.** Structures, hercs, flyers and ground vehicles can be added, each of a type fixed once it is added ([Per row](handoff-editor-authoring.md#per-row)). A herc added outside the squad, a flyer or a ground vehicle has no field of its own that changes after the add but its point, and its group none but its team, orders and, for a flyer group, height. An order added to a convoy is search/destroy on the squad, as for any group but the squad ("Orders"); the one retail convoy with later orders, `C2_08`'s two supply transports, guards and then travels.
- **The player's squad.** An overlay record cannot carry a field's unset value, so a field a variant supplies keeps the variant's value where an edit would unset it: after a chassis change on `TRAIN1` to `TRAIN4`, a slot past the new capacity keeps the variant's weapon and a mount that takes no guidance its ammunition type, neither of which the squad build or DBSIM reads. A launcher's mount whose stock fit carries the filler 5 is written SARH (0) instead, which DBSIM reads a 5 as ([`../retail/simulation/weapon-mounts.md`](../retail/simulation/weapon-mounts.md#the-join--mechloadout_constructweaponmounts-0040fff8)): the editor's choice, which leaves 0 in the generated record and the `player.mec` where the launch of an unedited stock fit holds 5. Removing a squadmate whose record draws a variant, as `TRAIN5`'s do, moves every later draw ("Conditions and variants" in [Holding the `.MSN` as the document](handoff-editor-authoring.md#holding-the-msn-as-the-document)).
- **Deleting.** Only roster objects, routes, trigger areas, actions, timers and objectives are deleted, and a group only with its last member: nothing deletes a group directly. A refused delete cannot be forced, and the view offers no way to clear the reference that refuses it.
- **Actions.** An action's text line (`0x44`) is not edited, and its records' conditions are not shown, so a field a conditioned record supplies holds an edit only in the states it survives in. A type change to 7 to 10 takes the first record of the kind as the target, whatever the action tested before. The engaged and defeated actions of a roster object whose variant supplies them cannot be taken away. The message choice lists what the mission's own `COMMAND<n>.STR` holds; a training mission's lines are its instructor's sentences, offered by id, each with the sentences after it joined as the training port shows them ([`../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md`](../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md#the-training-port)).
- **Routes.** Nothing sets a group's own route field ([Per row](handoff-editor-authoring.md#per-row)); a route made in the editor is walked once a group's first order names it ("Orders" in [In place](#in-place)), and an objective's condition 0 offers it once the subject's group has an order on it ("Objectives"). A waypoint is placed and inserted on the ground plane at Z 0 ("Waypoint height" above). A list entry naming a point the generation leaves out is not listed in the view, which edits the entries around it, and Insert after refuses a leg ending on one. A list a variant supplies cannot be given to the route alone.
- **Objectives.** A subject change leaves a condition-0 objective's route as it was, which the view then says never comes true when the new subject's group runs no order on it. A new objective goes last in row 17, so it is asked after every other, and nothing moves one; the first mandatory objective not yet true is the one whose failure text the status alert shows.
- **Text.** A line is edited in the state the generation shows: a conditioned replacement that fails in that state is neither shown nor written, and a line whose id has no record surviving in it shows a note in place of its field. Only the text package block 13 is written from, row 4's first record, is edited. Nothing checks a line's length: the mission screen's wrap asserts on a word wider than its box ([`../retail/shell/mission-screen.md`](../retail/shell/mission-screen.md#the-summary-text-box)), and the status alert's and F11 panel's labels are not measured. A line's trailing newline, which ends a paragraph on the mission screen, is kept as the record had it and cannot be added or taken away; a new line has none. The briefing movie is not set.
- **Framing.** A double-click on a link in the Properties panel selects without framing. A timer stands nowhere and is not framed.
- **Top-down view.** It is the perspective camera at its pitch limit, 88 degrees down rather than 90, not an orthographic map: things stand up from the ground toward the edges of the view, and a point's place on screen shifts with its height. It frames the zone's grid as it opens, not whatever window size it is later resized to.
- **Ground planes from high up.** A structure's ground plane (shadow, floor plates) is ranked against the terrain by the cell `HeightGrid.PickDrawCell` files it under in the visible region, which `TerrainVisibleRegion.Update` keeps from the last view whenever the view's frustum, cut off at the zone's visibility range, reaches no ground, as it may from the top-down view's height. How the planes draw there has not been looked at.

Counts for the retail missions: 58 of 62 use trigger areas, 257 distinct areas named 143 times as boxes and 132 as circles, 16 shared by more than one action. Subjects are the player in 142 of 338 actions and the player's group in 170. `C2_01`'s boundary strips and `TRAIN8.MSN`'s three defeat-chained reinforcement waves ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#train8msn-end-to-end)) are good test cases.

Outlining stands in for ghosting. Drawing the meshes translucent instead needs a tint or alpha path in `SceneRenderer`.

Size: small–medium.

### Action graph

The action view walks the wiring one link at a time; a node graph of the mission's actions, timers and the objects and groups between them would show it at once.

### Left out

- **Drop-pod and on-foot arrival points.** Picked at run time relative to the player's position and heading when the action fires; nothing static to draw.
- **Detection and engagement ranges** (50,000 and 100,000 units). Simulation constants, not mission data; a per-object debug ring at most.

## Gaps on the write path

### 1. Positions are indirect

An object does not hold a position; it holds a `PositionRef` index into block 1. Most retail roster records carry `-1` there, so the object inherits its group's point plus a formation offset ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule), rule 2, gives the ones that name their own), and moving one gives it a point of its own. `MissionDocument.PlacePoint` appends a row-6 record with a new GUID, which the generation exports last in block 1, so nothing shifts, or rewrites the point in place once the moving field is all that names its GUID. It does not look for an identical existing point to reuse: file length is not a constraint.

Points are shared on purpose. In 45 of the 62 retail missions a route waypoint is the same block-1 point as a trigger area's center or corner (94 of the 886 distinct waypoint points, from `HercWorks.Query routes` and `actions`): that is how a mission fires an action when a waypoint is reached. `C1_01`'s player route runs through points 119, 121, 122, 123 and 124, its drop-pod triggers are circles about 121 to 124, and the groups those triggers bring in deploy on 119. So a waypoint drag moves the shared point, and the trigger and the groups move with it; giving the waypoint a point of its own, which Alt+drag does, leaves them where they were. A group drag (block 11) and an area corner drag (block 4) make the same choice the same way.

### 2. Three consequences of appending

- **Block 1's extent frames the heads-down map** (see [`../retail/simulation/heads-down-display.md`](../retail/simulation/heads-down-display.md#the-maps-frame-of-reference), and `MissionBox`), and the mission box. A point outside the current box silently rescales the player's in-game map and moves the boundary warning and abort lines. The object view warns on it, and the mission box overlay is rebuilt after each edit.
- **File length is not a constraint.** `DBSim_LoadScriptDat` (`00424308`) reads through a `FileRStream` block by block and allocates each of blocks 1-6 and the group array from its own count, so a file longer than the retail 13,520 bytes reads correctly. Its fixed arrays are the per-roster slot flags and slot maps ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#the-two-pass-read--and-what-it-means-for-dbsim-keeps)); past them it writes over neighbouring globals. Appending block-1 points is unaffected; adding roster records is not: the mission check reports a roster past its cap, and the editor refuses to add a structure past block 9's, a herc past block 7's or a flyer past block 8's. VSHELL's own reader, `ShellMap_LoadScriptDat` (`004243d7`), has not been checked for caps, and it reads a save slot's file for the briefing map.
- **A mech's or flyer's own point has never been observed live.** No retail mech or flyer record names one ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule), rule 2), so `Mech_AttachToGroup` and `Flyer_AttachToGroup` honouring it is RE-derived and engine-implemented, and the editor's moves of either rely on it. Structures' is exercised by retail data. Confirming it means writing such a file and running retail DBSIM.

### 3. Moved is not the same as stays there

- `MissionPlacement` carries `FormationOffset` even when the record supplies its own position, and AI followers hold formation on their leader every tick. For a non-leader group member the edit sets a spawn position the AI may walk out of; the object view says so.
- A group with no point of its own stands on its route's first waypoint, so moving that waypoint, as a plain group drag does, moves the start of the route too. Given a point of its own (Alt+drag), it spawns there instead, its route unchanged.
- A paints-ground base group snaps to a fixed spot in its tile, so a small move does nothing and a larger one jumps a whole tile ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#the-anchor)); the base pad overlay shows the tile.
- A group waiting to deploy by drop pod or on foot is placed at a placeholder, so moving it changes nothing once the mission runs ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#arrival--group_deploymentcheck-004236c4)); its view says so. One arriving in place, and a held-back structure, stand where they are placed: the structure, solid and undrawn, from the first frame ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#held-back-structures-stand-from-the-start)).

### 4. Live scene refresh

An object move re-places that object's items and pick sphere. After every change to block 1, `PointFollowers` places again, by the loader's rules, whatever stands on a point that moved: route points, trigger areas (and a circle's radius, from block 4), each group's point and heading (its own point, else its route's first waypoint, facing along the first leg when it names no heading) with the members spread from it, a paints-ground group's pad outline, and objects whose roster record names the point. The positions it moves are editor copies in `MissionIndex`; `Mission` and the mission check stay as the mission was loaded or last rebuilt after an add or a delete (a `MissionPlacement.Position`, the orders' routes and the actions' areas go stale, and nothing in the editor reads a position from them). The ground is levelled and the pads painted again where the structures and pads now stand (`TerrainLeveller`).
