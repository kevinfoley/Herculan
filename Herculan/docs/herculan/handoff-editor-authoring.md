# Handoff — the `.MSN` document and mission authoring in the HERCULAN Editor

How the editor, `Herculan.Engine.Editor`, holds a mission as its `.MSN` and writes each edit, what building a mission from nothing still needs, and the scope and sequencing of that work. What the editor does now, and the consequences of its edits, are [`handoff-editor-mission-editing.md`](handoff-editor-mission-editing.md).

## Authoring a mission from scratch

Everything in [In place](handoff-editor-mission-editing.md#in-place) starts a new mission, moves something, changes a group's side, adds or deletes an object, creates, edits or deletes a route, edits a group's orders, edits the player's squad, or sets the theater and time of day; every other field of every record is shown read-only or not at all. This is what building a mission from nothing needs beyond that.

### The `.MSN` layer

The editor holds a VOL mission as its `.MSN` and `.ENG` and shows it through the `script.dat` generated from them, the one VSHELL writes once it has filtered and resolved the `.MSN`. What the `.MSN` holds beyond that, the editor neither shows nor edits:

| `.MSN` part | What it carries |
|---|---|
| [Row 1](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-conditions--row-1) | the campaign-flag comparisons and random draws every record's condition names |
| [Row 2](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-header-patch--row-2) | the header words, patched by condition, and flags to clear |
| Row 3 | the briefing and debrief movie ids |
| Row 4 | the objective, briefing and intelligence lines and the briefing movie. `script.dat` keeps only the objective lines, as block 13, which the outliner lists read-only |
| [Row 5](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#row-5--the-debrief) | the debrief lines and movie |
| [`.ENG`](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-eng-string-table) | every line of the mission's text, with conditioned replacements. The editor reads the generated `mission.str` and edits none of it |
| [Variants](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#variants) and [repeated GUIDs](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#repeated-guids) | random alternatives and condition-dependent overrides, resolved before `script.dat` is written |
| GUIDs | how `.MSN` records name each other, where `script.dat` names by array position |

A mission the shell loads, practice or campaign, is an `.MSN`. A practice mission needs at least row 2's header and group 0's row-12 squad (`TRAIN1` and `TRAIN3` carry no row 4), and an objective's failure text needs the `.ENG`; a campaign mission uses rows 1, 3, 4 and 5 and variants too. File > New mission writes the practice set ([In place](handoff-editor-mission-editing.md#in-place), "New mission").

### Holding the `.MSN` as the document

The `.MSN` and its `.ENG` are the editor's document, `MissionDocument` (`Data/File/Msn/MissionDocument.cs`), with `script.dat` a view generated from them after each edit. Paths are under `src/HercWorks.Core/` unless named otherwise.

**The model.** `MissionFile` (`Data/File/Msn/MissionFile.cs`) holds all 17 rows, and `MissionFileTransformer` reads and writes them in file order (`Io/Transform/Common/MissionFileTransformer.cs:11-45`, `:312-340`). Rows 1, 3, 4 and 6 to 17 are typed records whose fields are settable properties (`:57-306`), every GUID-keyed one a `MapObject` with a settable `GUID` (`Data/File/Msn/MapObject.cs:7-10`). Row 2 is a raw `short[41]`, `MissionSettingsPatch.Data` (`Data/File/Msn/MissionSettingsPatch.cs:31-33`): its layout (a condition, ten header words, thirty flag indices) is what the generator reads (`Io/Transform/Common/MissionGenerator.cs:345-378`), but nothing names the words. Row 5 is raw bytes, `MissionFile.DebriefBytes` (`MissionFile.cs:33`, transformer `:27-28`): the debrief reload reads each 32-word record as a condition, thirty text refs and a row-3 ref (`MissionGenerator.cs:150-179`), and no type does. Inside the typed rows some spans stay unnamed words or arrays (`Unk04`, `Unk06`, `Unk1A`, row 12's and row 16's `DeadZone`, row 13's `FlagSpan`), and row 10's counter pairs are a flat `short[20]`. The `.ENG` is `MissionStringFile`, its entries settable (`Data/File/Msn/MissionStringFile.cs:44-72`), and its writer takes each length from the text (`Io/Transform/Common/MissionStringFileTransformer.cs:36-55`). `HercWorks.Query census` round-trips all 62 `.MSN` and all 62 `.ENG` files in `ZONES.VOL` byte for byte, `DEMO2.MSN`'s truncated row 17 included (a null in `MissionFile.Objectives`, `MissionFile.cs:68-78`). A row is a fixed array, so adding a record replaces the row's array. `MissionFile`'s lookups (`GetPoint`, `GetMech` and the rest, `MissionFile.cs:80-108`) ignore conditions and variants and merge no overlays, so they are not what a mission resolves to; only the generator gives that.

**Provenance.** `MissionGenerator.Load` takes the `.MSN` as bytes, not a `MissionFile` (`MissionGenerator.cs:103`), and walks them with its own cursor, so an edited `MissionFile` is written before it is generated from. Both read the rows in the same order, so the loop counter of each `Read…` method is the record's index in `MissionFile`'s row array, a record that fails its condition included. `MissionGenerator.Provenance`, a `MissionProvenance` (`Io/Transform/Common/MissionProvenance.cs`), is told by the generator as it adds, replaces, overlays and variant-copies, and answers which records supplied what it writes. `Slot(block, index)` gives an exported `script.dat` record's `SlotProvenance`: the `MsnRecordId` (row and index) that created the slot, the records merged into it in read order, each `VariantCopy` (the record that asked, the source it copied from and the words covered), and `Word(word)`, per `.MSN` word, a `WordSource` naming the record whose write the slot holds and, for a copied word, the variant source it was authored in. `ExportedSlot(record)` is the reverse, the `ScriptDatSlot` a record created or was merged into, null for a record that failed its condition, has GUID `-1` or is a variant source. `Lines` and `LineOf(engRecord)` do the same for `mission.str` lines: the `.ENG` record that placed each and the one whose text it holds. `HeaderPatches` lists the row-2 records whose condition passed, and `HeaderWord(word)` names the one whose write a header word holds, null when none sets it. It covers blocks 1 to 12, block 13 as row 4's slot 0, the header and every `mission.str` line; row 3's values, which reach only the row-4 and row-5 movie ids, are not traced, and a row-6 point with its sum flag set reports its coordinates as its own record's. Row 8's word 4 stands for its waypoint count and list together. It changes nothing the generator writes, which its reproduction of two retail TRAIN5 handoffs keeps checked.

**Conditions and variants.** Every record passes `Gate` (`:251-263`) against the row-1 survivors. Row 1's type 0 compares the caller's flag array (`:200-208`), type 1 draws from the caller's roll (`:210-215`) and type 2 tests its parent's draw (`:217-222`); `PickVariant` draws again for every record that asks, in file order (`:290-312`). Row 2 zeroes flags in the caller's array and writes into the caller's clear list (`:345-378`), so each generation needs fresh copies of both. The editor's VOL open goes through `MissionFileLaunch.Open` (`src/Herculan.Engine/Shell/MissionFileLaunch.cs`): a practice mission's flags are cleared and flags 3 to 6 seeded (`ShellTrainingLaunch.SeedDrawnFlags`), a campaign mission's start empty and take the career position's seed, and every draw comes from `ShellTrainingLaunch.StartupRandom`, seeded from the tick count (`src/Herculan.Engine/Shell/ShellTrainingLaunch.cs`), so opening the same mission twice can pick different variants. A practice load then overwrites header words 0 and 5 to 9 with theater 1 and the practice options (`ShellTrainingLaunch.PracticeHeader`), and a campaign load words 0, 5, 6 and 7 (`ShellCampaignLaunch.Write`); the editor's generation of a practice load leaves words 0 and 9 to row 2 (`MissionOpener`, `MissionGeneration.WithoutOverwrite`). `HercWorks.Query lint` uses zero flags and a roll of 0 throughout (`src/HercWorks.Query/LintQuery.cs:22-23`).

A generation (`Data/File/Msn/MissionGeneration.cs`) takes the flags the load was handed, before its row 2 cleared any, a fresh clear list for each run, the header words the load wrote over, and draws from a copy of VSHELL's generator as it stood when the load began drawing, copied again for every run (`SimRandom.Copy`). Each generation therefore draws what the handoff's load drew, so the squad its `player.mec` was built for is the document's, and nothing in the scene reshuffles on an edit. No command picks a new seed, and no flag state can be chosen. Draws are made in file order, row 1's type-1 records first, then each variant-keyed record of rows 8, 12, 13 and 14 as it is read (the keys of rows 6, 7 and 15 are `-1` in every retail file). An edit that appends records with no variant key, as every add does, leaves every draw as it was; deleting a keyed record, or adding a draw ahead of others, moves the draws after it. A roll of 0 throughout is stable under every edit but shows one outcome only.

An edit writes the record that supplied the value in the editor's state:

| The `script.dat` value came from | The edit writes |
|---|---|
| a row-3, 6, 7, 8 or 9 GUID carried by more than one surviving record (replace) | the last surviving record of the GUID, whose payload the slot holds. 451 row-6 GUIDs in 24 missions are carried by two or more unconditioned records (`HercWorks.Query repeats`) |
| a row-10 to 16 GUID carried by more than one surviving record (overlay) | per field, the last surviving record of the GUID that sets it, else the first |
| a field of rows 12 to 15 copied from a variant | not the source, a GUID `-1` alternative shared by every record that can draw it, but a record of the same GUID appended to the row, carrying that field alone, under the condition of the record that drew the variant, so it overlays the field whatever is drawn in every state that record survives in. An unconditioned one would create the slot alone, every other field unset, in a state where every other record of the GUID fails. `MissionDocument.SetField` writes one for rows 12 to 14 |
| a row-8 waypoint list copied from a variant (2% of retail routes) | the alternative, changing every route that draws it (`MissionDocument.SetWaypoint`, `SetWaypoints`). Detaching the route instead, its key set to `-1` and its own list written, would take a draw away and move every later one. Row 8 replaces rather than overlays, so an appended record cannot carry one waypoint |
| a row-15 order another group slot names too (7 of the 609 order GUIDs the retail groups name) | a copy of every record of the GUID under a new GUID, named in the edited slot, and the field in the copy (`GroupOrders`), so the other slots keep the order as it was |
| a field set to its unset value (a member slot to `-1`) | every record of the GUID that sets the field, whatever its condition: an overlay cannot carry an unset value |
| a header word, which row 2's records overlay into one header | as for an overlay: the last surviving row-2 record that sets it, else the first surviving one, else a row-2 record appended unconditioned that sets nothing else; its unset value (theater 1, day) in every row-2 record that sets it, whatever its condition (`MissionDocument.SetHeaderWord`) |

A supplier with a condition holds the edit only in the states it survives in, which no view says yet. Counting what shares a point counts every record naming its GUID, whatever its condition, since one conditioned out in the editor's state names it in another.

**Regenerating on each edit.** Each `MissionDocument` edit writes the `MissionFile`, keeping copies of the rows it changed for undo (`MsnEdit`), then writes the file, generates from it, parses the output as a `ScriptDat` and copies its header and blocks into `MissionDocument.Script`, the one `ScriptDat` everything reading the document holds (`PointFollowers`, the tools' own checks). Edits made inside one `MissionDocument.Change` come back as one `MsnEdit`, and are all put back when one of them fails. Undo and Redo write a step's rows back and generate again. Writing, generating and parsing touch arrays of a few hundred records; the scene's terrain and models are not reloaded, except that a change of theater or time of day builds the models and the terrain mesh again ([In place](handoff-editor-mission-editing.md#in-place), "The mission's theater and time of day"). A move and a side change keep every generated record where it was, so the incremental scene updates apply as they are. A Save writes the `.MSN` and the `.ENG` as they stand, and an export writes the generated `script.dat` and `mission.str`; neither builds anything.

**Rebuilding after an edit.** A delete moves every later generated record of its block down one, an add's records are exported last, a route edit does either to block 1 or block 3, a squad edit changes the squad the launch builds, and an order edit changes which route each group walks and what the mission check finds, so after any of them, and after the undo or redo of any, `MissionRebuild` (`src/Herculan.Engine.Editor/MissionRebuild.cs`) builds the editor's state again from the generated file. For a mission loaded in training mode it first writes the handoff's `player.mec` again, as the launch would write it for the generation (`MissionSquad.PlayerMec`: `ShellTrainingLaunch.NewCareer` drawing again from VSHELL's generator as it stood when the open began, `ShellTrainingLaunch.BuildSquad`, the training load's squad half, over the document's `MissionDocument.Generator`, and the export); a campaign mission's squad is the career's and stays. Then it builds a `Mission` (`MissionLoader.Load(content, script, path)`), `MissionIndex`, the pick spheres, the overlays' lines, `PointFollowers` and the mission check, which therefore runs again then and only then. Each placement stands on the scene object already built for the `.MSN` record that created its slot, matched by GUID through provenance, by type and by weapon fit, since a mech's loadout is built with its object, and the player's squad by its order in `player.mec`; its `SceneObject.Placement` is pointed at the new indices. A placement with none gets a new object from `MissionScene.CreateObject`, and an object no placement names leaves the drawing. No object is discarded, so an undone delete stands the same object, model and drawing where it stood, and terrain and models are not loaded again. The selection survives when what it names does: an object by identity, or else as the object now standing for the same record, which a chassis change builds; a group or a route by its GUID, a route's waypoint while the route still has one there; and is cleared otherwise. A step of the undo history that holds block indices, a move's object or a side change's group, needs no rewriting: the history is undone last first, so a step only ever runs against the `.MSN` it was made on, whose generation gives the same indices, and every step that adds or removes records rebuilds as it is undone or redone.

**A plain `script.dat`.** The editor edits `.MSN` missions only. `DATA\script.dat` and a save slot's `sav\script%d.dat` have no `.MSN` beside them, and none can be regenerated reliably from one: `DATA\script.dat` does not record its mission, and a regeneration's campaign flags and draws need not be the ones it was written under. The editor opens such a file read-only, for inspection: every edit tool refuses (`PointMover.WhyReadOnly`) and its controls are disabled, the Toolbox adds nothing, Undo and Redo stay empty, and Save, Save As and Export are disabled. No view or menu repeats why: "SCRIPT.DAT READ-ONLY" stands once in the 3D viewport's top-right corner, in large bold dark yellow-grey (`EditorText.Reason` leaves the read-only reason out of every note).

Checked and not found:

- No `HercWorks.Query` report counts the row-10 to 16 GUIDs carried by more than one record; `repeats` covers rows 3 and 6 to 9. How often an overlay edit meets a conditioned supplier in retail is not counted.
- Whether every retail variant table has a child whose range holds a draw of 0 was not checked. A table with none makes `PickVariant` answer `-1` (`MissionGenerator.cs:306-311`), and a row-12 to 15 record that asked keeps its own refs unresolved (`:805-818`), which matters for a roll of 0.
- How long one write, generation and parse takes was not measured.
- The slot caps are checked against the file generated in the editor's state; no check covers the most records any flag state lets survive.

### Mission level

- **The header** ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#header-format)): the theater (offset 0, which picks `wld\world<theater * 2 + variant>`: the terrain texture bank, the palette, the sky and the fog, [`../retail/formats/wld-world.md`](../retail/formats/wld-world.md)) and the day or night variant (offset 18) are chosen in the New mission dialog and changed at any time after, shown live ([In place](handoff-editor-mission-editing.md#in-place), "The mission's theater and time of day"). The zone is set once by File > New mission, and nothing changes it, the objective type (offset 6, which picks the player's objective arm) or the training mission number after. The difficulty and the two training cheats are overwritten at every shell load.

  Every retail load writes over the theater, a campaign's from its stage and a practice or `INSTANT ACTION` one as 1, and a practice or `INSTANT ACTION` load writes the practice screen's time of day over the variant; only a campaign mission plays its `.MSN`'s own day or night. The editor shows a campaign mission as the game plays it, on its stage's theater by its own day or night, and every other mission on its own theater and by its own time of day, which the planned menu for playing user-made missions is to honour: this engine's departure from `INSTANT ACTION`, which the user chose. The game's own launches still write theater 1 and the practice screen's time of day.
- **The mission list.** File > Load from VOL lists each mission by its file name and whether it is saved or new; it is to show each one's zone and theater as well.

### Counters and caps

- **Mission counters.** Actions, objectives and out-of-action reports write counters, which persist into the campaign flags the next mission's row 1 tests ([`../retail/shell/campaign-loop.md`](../retail/shell/campaign-loop.md#the-campaign-flag-array-is-the-msn-condition-store)). Nothing lists which counters a mission writes, so nothing helps choose a free one or wire it to a condition.
- **Slot caps.** Adding flyers needs the refusal structure and herc adds have, against `ScriptDatLint`'s 50 flyer slots.

## Suggested sequencing

What a new mission needs to become a mission, in order:

1. **Next:** flyers, added in groups with a first order as hercs are, then ground vehicles.
2. Objectives with their text: the objective list, failure text and briefing.
3. Trigger areas, their corner moves through the same point placement included, actions and timers.
4. The campaign layer (conditions, variants, debrief) last.
