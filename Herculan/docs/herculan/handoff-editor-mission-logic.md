# Handoff — orders, triggers, countdowns, objectives and text in the editor

A group's orders, the triggers and countdowns that drive a mission, the wiring between them, the campaign variables a mission sets, objectives and the mission's text. Part of the [editor handoff](handoff-editor.md). Which records each tool writes is in [The tools](handoff-editor-document.md#the-tools).

The editor's UI calls a row-10 action a **trigger**, a row-11 action timer a **countdown**, and a row-17 objective a **win condition** (mandatory) or a **lose condition** (any other value), in every label, link and refusal reason; the code keeps the retail names (`ActionEditor`, `TimerEditor`, `ActionSelection`, `ObjectiveEditor`).

## Names

Every record a view, link, picker, outliner row, delete blocker or the Toolbox mentions is named by what it is and does, from the layout (`MissionNames`), with its number in its row dimmed after the text so a link still reads ("(group 5)", "(trigger 4)"); the number is its place among its row's records counted from 1 (`MissionText.Number`, the user's decision), as the outliner, the scene's labels and the overlays number it: the squad is first in row 16 and named without a number, so the group after it is group 2. Nothing is matched against the file through it. A name is drawn on one line, cut with "..." to fit and whole on hover (`NameText`), never wrapped in a list or picker, and a picker's list opens at least as wide as the Properties panel. There are no editor-only names (the user's choice).

| Record | Named by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| group | team, roster (hercs, flyers, structures, or ground vehicles when every member drives) and members, each type once with a count; the squad "Player's squad", with no number | "Cybrid hercs: SCARAB x2, DIABLO (group 5)" |
| group in a sentence or a reference list | team and roster alone | "Cybrid hercs (group 5)", "the player's squad" |
| herc, flyer, structure or ground vehicle | its type (`MECHS.NAM`, `FLYERS.NAM`, or the name the MFD's F5 screen prints), "2 of 3" in member order when its group has several of that type, and its group; a squad HERC as the player's or a squadmate's, by its slot | "SCARAB 2 of 3 in Cybrid hercs (group 5)", "OUTLAW, the player's HERC" |
| a roster record no group places | its type, as plain text, not a link | "SCARAB, in no group: never in the mission" |
| trigger | what fires it (who enters which areas, else the first countdown or object firing it) and its first kind of effect, ", and more" when it has others | "The player enters area 0, 1 or 2: brings in 3 groups (trigger 0)", "Countdown 2 runs out: moves Cybrid hercs (group 4) on (trigger 6)" |
| countdown | its delay and what starts it | "Countdown: 30 s after the player enters area 3 (countdown 2)", "Countdown: 30 s from mission start" |
| trigger area | its shape and whose entry its first testing trigger tests | "Circle entered by the player's squad (area 3)", "Box that no trigger tests" |
| route | its number, then who walks it (team and roster alone) and whether it loops | "Route 2 (Cybrid hercs, loop)", "Route 1 (player's squad)", "Route 3 (not walked)" |
| objective | its sentence ([Objectives](#objectives)) | "Must happen: Cybrid hercs (group 4) are written off (objective 1)" |

A name refers to an area, a countdown or a route inside another name by its number alone ("area 3"), since naming it in full would loop (an area by its trigger, the trigger by its area).

## Orders

Every group's view lists its filled order slots (`OrderSlotsView`, through `OrderEditor`), the player's squad's included, whose first order's route is the player's route. It reads the layout by the group's GUID, so it shows the orders of any group of the `.MSN`, one the version of the mission the editor shows leaves out included (that group's view shows its title, Present when and orders). Each slot is "1st order", "2nd order" and so on, with Up, Down and Remove, and offers:

- **Does**: Search and destroy, Ram, Guard, Patrol, Sleep, Travel or Follow (verbs 0 to 6), each with what it does and when it is done, from the state it installs and `Group_IsOrderComplete`'s test ([`../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md#when-an-order-is-finished--group_isordercomplete-004239fc), [`../retail/simulation/ai-navigation.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-navigation.md), [`../retail/simulation/ai-combat-states.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-combat-states.md)): on hover in the list, and under the choice for the one chosen;
- **Route**, any route of the mission or none, by name, with a link. The first order's is the route the group walks. A later order's moves nothing, as the note under it says ("No effect on movement: a group only walks its first order's route."): its only reader is an objective asking whether the group has finished the first of its orders on that route ([Objectives](#objectives)), so the choice is enabled only when such an objective reads this slot, and each one is listed under it as a link (the editor's choice);
- **Target**: none, a group, a herc or a structure, by name (a flyer, which no retail order names, is not offered), with a link;
- **Ends when**: the trigger that ends it ([Wiring](#wiring)); with none on a Ram or Sleep order that has a next one, a note says the group never reaches it, as neither verb is ever done by itself;
- its Present when ([Present when](handoff-editor-conditions.md#present-when)).

Add an order fills the slot after the last, Remove moves every later order up, and Up and Down trade places, so the slots stay filled from slot 0: `Group_OrderTick` moves on only to a next slot holding an order. A new order is the editor's choice of a retail shape (`GroupOrders.NewOrder`), on slot 0's route:

- the squad: patrol, no subject (36 of the 63 slot-0 orders of the retail squad's records);
- a convoy: travel, no subject (11 of the 12 retail convoys' slot-0 orders; `C2_08`'s two supply transports, the one retail convoy with later orders, guard and then travel);
- any other group: a new herc group's first order on its team ([Hercs](handoff-editor-roster.md#hercs)), Search and destroy on the squad for a Cybrid group, Patrol with no target for a Human one.

7 of the 609 order GUIDs the retail groups name are named by two or three groups, each in the same slot, none within one group. Editing a slot whose order another slot names first gives it its own copy, which the view does not mention, since the edit changes only this group either way. A removed order's records go only when no slot names it any more.

A Search and destroy order whose target is on the group's own team, a group outside the squad's, gets a red note under its Target: it never attacks that target and goes after no other enemy. The mission check finds the same ([Mission check](handoff-editor-viewing.md#mission-check)).

An edit that changes which route slot 0 runs, a route chosen for it, an Up or Down that moves another order into it, or a remove of the first order, also writes the new route into the group's own route field (row 16 `0x36`) when that field named the old one, as 377 of the 433 retail herc group records keep the two the same (`GroupOrders.FollowFirstRoute`, the editor's choice); so the one-waypoint route a new group stood on, once its first order walks another, is referenced by nothing and can be deleted.

Shapes no retail order has are not made:

- a first order with no route in a group with no point of its own, which stands on that route (each of the 35 retail group records whose slot-0 order names no route names a point, 8 of them no heading): its Route offers no None, with the reason;
- Search and destroy, Ram or Follow with no target (all 385, 17 and 9 retail orders of those verbs name one, and `Group_IsOrderComplete` reads search/destroy's): a change to one of them on an order with no target gives it the squad as target, as the view notes, and Target offers no None.

Removing a group's last order is refused, with the reason, where DBSIM faults without one: a group with no heading of its own, and a herc group other than the squad (the mission check's two group findings). A flyer group keeps its last order too, since every retail one has one and what its AI does with none is not established (the editor's rule). A route an order names cannot be deleted.

Not edited: an order's formation and point.

## Wiring

Three records wait on a trigger, each through a field of its own, and each is set from both ends: from the record's view, and from the trigger's Then list ([Triggers](#triggers)).

- **A group** offers the trigger it waits on ("Waits on"), with how it then arrives; the squad has none.
- **A herc, flyer, structure or ground vehicle** outside the squad offers the trigger it fires when engaged and when defeated ("When engaged, fires", "When defeated, fires"), a flyer's engaged one included, which DBSIM reads though no retail flyer sets it. The note under them gives the engagement distance in metres (`Detection.EngagementRange`, 300 m) ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#an-objects-own-two-actions--0x1b2-and-0x1b6)).
- **An order slot** offers the trigger that ends it ("Ends when").

Each is None or any trigger of the layout by its name ([Names](#names)) (`ActionChoice`), with a link, read from the layout by the record's GUID: the group's waiting trigger from its base, the object's two from its roster record's, the order's from its row-15 record's; a ref to a trigger the layout has none for reads as none, as the generation resolves it. A field a template supplies offers no None, since an overlay cannot carry the unset value, so a template-supplied engaged or defeated trigger cannot be taken away.

## Campaign variable writes

A trigger, an objective, and a herc, flyer, structure, ground vehicle or group put out of action each set up to ten campaign variables, the array a later mission's [Present when](handoff-editor-conditions.md#present-when) tests ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#the-mission-counters--dat_004a9ef4)). Every view lists them the same way (`CampaignVariableWrites`), one sentence per write: "Add 1 to campaign variable 100", "Set campaign variable 100 to 0", "Subtract 1 from campaign variable 100", with the variable's meaning after its number where the game sets or reads it ([the picker](handoff-editor-conditions.md#the-campaign-variable-picker)): "Add 1 to campaign variable 22 (ATC50 units the debrief adds to the armory)". A code the record's layer has no case for reads "Unknown operation on campaign variable N (no effect in play)". The three layers number their operations differently, and a list offers only its own:

| Writes | Heading | Operations, by code |
|---|---|---|
| a trigger ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#the-four-ways-an-action-activates)) | Sets campaign variables | Add 1 (6), Set to 0 (5) |
| an objective, win or lose condition alike, the first time its condition holds ([`../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md#the-record)) | The first time it comes true, sets campaign variables | Set to 1 (4), Set to 0 (5), Add 1 (6), Subtract 1 (7) |
| a herc, the first time it is destroyed or immobilized ([`../retail/simulation/component-damage.md`](../retail/simulation/component-damage.md#going-out-of-the-fight)) | When destroyed or immobilized, sets campaign variables | Set to 0 (1), Add 1 (2), Set to 1 to 4 (`0x0d` to `0x10`) ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#the-out-of-action-report)) |
| a flyer, when its component 0 goes; a structure or a ground vehicle, when it is destroyed, a ground vehicle sharing the structure's damage path ([`../retail/simulation/structure-behaviour.md`](../retail/simulation/structure-behaviour.md#taking-damage--base_applydamage-00404d70)) | When destroyed, sets campaign variables | as a herc's |
| a group, when its last member to go finds every other destroyed or immobilized, a disarmed one counting as standing | When every member is destroyed or immobilized, sets campaign variables | as a herc's |

In the trigger and objective views a write is a sentence, then a choice of the layer's operations and a variable from the picker's list for a write, with Remove; Add follows the last, up to ten, and the list stays filled from the first. Choosing a variable keeps the operation, or makes it Add 1 when the layer has no case for it. A new trigger write is campaign variable 0, Add 1, as all 66 retail action writes are, each sending the trigger's message ([Triggers](#triggers)); a new objective write is campaign variable 52, Add 1, the first write of 4 of the 31 retail objectives that write one. An objective's list with no write says "It sets no campaign variable."; a trigger's Then list shows its writes only when it sets one, and its Add... menu's Set a campaign variable adds the first.

The other three are read-only and shown only when they list a write. A HERC of the player's squad sets none of its own in play, since its slots start empty ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#the-out-of-action-report)): where its record lists writes, its view says "None in play" under the heading instead.

## Triggers

**New trigger**, at the top of the outliner's Triggers list, and **New trigger testing this area**, in a trigger area's view, add one and select it. The trigger view (`TriggerView`, through `ActionEditor`) reads the layout and `MissionIndex` by GUID, so it shows any trigger of the `.MSN`, and reads top to bottom as "when this happens, do that":

- **Fires when**, for a trigger with an area, one choice of the eleven kinds `Actions_EvaluateTriggers` has a case for, each worded by whose position its areas test: "The player's HERC", "Any HERC of the player's squad", "Any member of a human (Cybrid, herc, flyer, structure) group that has arrived", "A particular herc" (flyer, "structure or ground vehicle"), "Any member of a particular group" ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#trigger-areas--actions_evaluatetriggers-00426b70)). The five group kinds note that a group still waiting to arrive is not tested. For the four particular kinds a second choice under it picks which one, from the layout's records of that row by name ([Names](#names)), with a link; "No target chosen." in bold when it has none. A change to a particular kind takes the first layout entry of that row as target, whatever the trigger tested before, and is refused when the mission has none; a change to any other kind unsets it, as 334 retail records have it (the editor's choices). A stored kind with no case reads "An unknown kind (12)", with its value.
- **enters one of these areas**, up to four, each a choice of the mission's areas with Remove and a link, and Add an area after the last; a remove moves later areas up, since the load stops at the first `-1`, and an area already tested is refused. Slots 4 to 7, which no retail record fills, stay `-1`.
- **Also fired by**: the countdowns whose fired list holds it ("when it runs out") and the objects that fire it when engaged or defeated, as links. They come from `MissionIndex.Links`, which `TriggerWiring` builds from the layout's base fields: every countdown, every placed herc, flyer, structure and ground vehicle outside the squad (a squad HERC is built from `player.mec` and fires nothing of its record's, and a record no group places is never spawned), every group's waiting trigger and every order slot.
- **Fires when**, for a trigger with no area, says what fires it in place of the three above, since DBSIM tests whose position only on a trigger's areas ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#trigger-areas--actions_evaluatetriggers-00426b70)): each countdown firing it as its delay after the trigger that starts it, "30 s after" a trigger link "fires, when" a countdown link "runs out" (or "after the mission starts"), and each object firing it as its link and "is engaged" or "is defeated". With nothing firing it, a bold note says so and names the three places that can. **Add an area** follows; once the trigger has an area the view is as above. The stored kind and target are kept as they are.
- **Then**, with **Add...** beside it, a menu of Bring in a group, Move a group on from its order, Start a countdown (New countdown, or one of the mission's), Set a campaign variable and Send a message. An entry that links another record to the trigger is written in that record, through the same edit its own view makes (`TriggerFollowers`): a group's deployment action (`ActionWiring.SetDeployment`), an order slot's ending action (`GroupOrders.SetAction`, which copies an order another slot shares first) or a countdown's starting action (`TimerEditing.SetStart`). A group's or an order's Remove unsets that one field, with the consequence on hover: the group is then in the mission from the start, the order no longer ends on a trigger. A countdown's Remove deletes it (below). Each record in the menu is by its name ([Names](#names)); one already wired to another trigger says so ("now waits on trigger 2"); choosing it moves it to this one. The entries, each shown only when there is one:
  - **Brings in**: each group waiting on it, a link with Remove, and under them **Arrives**: "By drop pod, in front of the player" (within 90° of the player's heading), "By drop pod, straight ahead of the player" (within 22.5°), "On foot, from behind the player", "On foot, from ahead of the player" or "In place" (verbs 2, 3, 4, 5 and 1), its tooltip saying how far from the player and on what bearing it arrives, or that it goes live where it stands ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#arrival--group_deploymentcheck-004236c4)). The arrival is one field of the trigger, so the tooltip also says every group it brings in arrives the same way. `Group_DeploymentCheck` treats every value but 2 to 5 as in place, so a stored 0 or a value past 5 shows as In place, a value past 5 with a note giving it, and choosing In place over either writes nothing (the editor's choice).
  - **Moves these groups on to their next order**: each order slot ending on it, as "Cybrid hercs (group 3), from its 1st order (Search and destroy)", with Remove.
  - **Starts these countdowns**: each countdown it starts. One with a trigger of its own ([Countdowns](#countdowns)) is a block, open by default and folding: "Countdown", a link, its delay as a field in seconds, with hours and minutes after it from a minute up, and Remove; under it "when it runs out:" with Add... and that trigger's Then list, drawn as this one is, so a countdown in it nests again, two blocks deep at most, which is what the panel's width holds (the training missions chain countdowns up to eight deep). A deeper countdown, and one whose trigger is already open further up, is shown in the other form. Any other countdown is its link with Remove, its delay as a field and the triggers it fires, as links. New countdown makes, as one edit, a countdown started by the trigger, of 10 s as one made from the outliner, and a trigger with no area that it fires, and stays in the trigger's view (`DelayedActions.Create`; the editor's choice).
    Remove deletes the countdown, as one edit (`DelayedActions.Remove`); one with a trigger of its own takes that trigger with it: the groups it brings in are then in the mission from the start, the orders it ends no longer end on a trigger, the countdowns it starts are removed by the same rule, and its writes and message go with it. When that does more than delete a trigger that does nothing, a confirmation, "Remove the countdown?", names the groups, the orders and the countdowns inside it, says when writes or messages go too, and offers Remove and Cancel. It is refused, with the reason, while a template of a random table or an order no group runs references a trigger it would delete. Hovering Remove says what it deletes.
  - **Sets campaign variables**, up to ten ([Campaign variable writes](#campaign-variable-writes)).
  - **Sends a message**: a choice of the lines of the `COMMAND<n>.STR` the mission speaks from, `n` its header's training mission number (row 2's base, `TriggerMessages`), which no launch writes over ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#the-training-mission-number)), each offered by its text cut to fit, the whole line under the choice when it is cut, with Remove; written as its id plus one in `0x4E`. A training mission's lines are its instructor's sentences, each with the sentences after it joined as the training port shows them ([`../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md`](../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md#the-training-port)). The note says HQ sends it on the pilot and squad channel (a training mission's instructor shows it) once for each campaign variable the trigger sets, and in bold that it is never sent when the trigger sets none ([`../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md`](../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md#its-speakerless-set)).
- **Delete**, refused while a countdown starts on or fires it, a group waits on it, an object fires it or an order ends on it, in any record whatever its condition: each a link by its name ([Names](#names)): a countdown, a group by its team and roster ("Cybrid hercs (group 12)"), an object ("SCARAB in Cybrid hercs (group 5)"), an order ("2nd order of Cybrid hercs (group 4)"), and counts of the options of random tables and the orders no group runs that reference it.
- **Advanced**, collapsed and shown only when there is something under it: the text ref at `0x44` as "Leftover text (no effect in play): ...", which one retail record sets and DBSIM drops, and the message's id and file.

A trigger has no Present when: no retail row-10 record carries a condition ([Present when](handoff-editor-conditions.md#present-when)). A trigger area's view lists each trigger testing it the same way read-only, its Then list as one line per entry, a countdown's own trigger's list under "when it runs out:" beneath it (`TriggerView.DrawSummary`). After each edit every area is colored again by what tests it.

Gaps: the leftover text is not edited, and a trigger's records under a condition other than its first record's (none in retail) are not shown, so an edit writes the base.

## Countdowns

**New countdown**, at the top of the outliner's Countdowns list, adds one and selects it; a trigger's Add... menu adds one that trigger starts, with a trigger of its own ([Triggers](#triggers)).

A countdown's **own trigger** is the one trigger it fires when nothing else can fire that trigger: no area, no object engaged or defeated, no other countdown, and not the trigger that starts the countdown, in any record whatever its condition (`DelayedActions.Of`). In retail, every trigger a countdown fires is fired by that countdown alone and tests no area, and no countdown fires two (`DelayedActions`). The trigger's Then list draws such a countdown with its own trigger's list inside it, and removes the two together ([Triggers](#triggers)).

The countdown view (`CountdownView`, through `TimerEditor`) reads the layout by GUID and offers:

- its title, its name ([Names](#names)): "Countdown: 30 s after the player enters area 3 (countdown 2)" or "Countdown: 30 s from mission start";
- **Starts when**: "The mission starts" or a trigger, with a link;
- **Delay (s)**, 0 to 32767: `ActionTimer_SetDelay` (`004679c0`) sign-extends the stored word into its 32-bit countdown, so a larger one would read as negative and fire at once;
- **When it runs out, fires**: each trigger it fires, a choice with Remove and a link, and Add a trigger after the last, up to ten: New trigger first, a trigger with no area added as one edit, staying on the countdown (`DelayedActions.AddNew`), then the mission's triggers. Only filled slots are shown, a record with a gap included; an add goes after the last filled slot and a remove moves the later ones up, so an edited list stays filled from the first (DBSIM fires every set slot);
- Delete, never refused, since nothing references a countdown.

## Objectives

The outliner lists the objectives under **Win and lose conditions**, each row its sentence, with **New win condition** at the top; the text package's objective lines are listed apart, under **Objective list the player reads (F11)**, with the note "These lines are only text: the mission never ticks them off. Win and lose conditions decide it.", since nothing reconciles the two ([`../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md#the-objectives-panel)) and they are edited in the Mission Settings window ([The mission's text](#the-missions-text)). New win condition adds one whose subject is the selection, and **Add a win condition** under "Win and lose conditions about it" in a group's or object's view adds one about it; both are disabled, with the reason, for any other selection, a flyer among them, which no retail objective asks about (the user's choice). A new objective (the editor's default) is a win condition, condition 1 (the subject is destroyed or written off), with an empty status alert text the author fills in; it is selected.

An objective is named by one sentence: "Must happen" for a win condition (`+0x00` is 1), "Must not happen" for a lose condition (any other value), then its subject and what happens to it, "Must happen: Cybrid hercs (group 4) are written off", "Must not happen: SUPPLY DEPOT in Human structures (group 1) is destroyed or immobilized", "Must happen: the player completes a data link" for the conditions that test the player alone. A finished order is said of the group whose order it reads: "Must happen: the player's squad finishes its order on route 1".

The objective view (`ObjectiveView`, through `ObjectiveEditor`) reads the layout by its row-17 index, so it shows any objective of the `.MSN`, and offers:

- its title, "Win condition" or "Lose condition" with its number, then its sentence, and its Present when;
- **Role**: "Must happen (win condition)" or "Must not happen (lose condition)", with what each does to the mission: it is won once every win condition holds, no lose condition does and no enemy nearby is aware of the player; it is lost once a lose condition holds and no enemy nearby is aware of the player ([`../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md#the-status--mission_status-004135e8));
- **Who**: a group, a herc or a structure by name, with a link (boxed in the scene); a record holding a flyer shows it by name, and a kind no case reads says so;
- **What happens**: what `Mission_EvaluateObjectives` asks, worded for a group ("Are written off") or an object ("Is destroyed or immobilized"), each with what it means in play on hover and under the choice, the clear-of-threats distances in metres ([What each condition asks](../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md#what-each-condition-asks)). Codes 4 and 10 ask what 3 and 9 ask, so the list offers 3 and 9 alone and a record holding 4 or 10 shows as the one it repeats (the editor's choice). The data link conditions say that only a mission whose start settings choose the data link ever completes one ([The player think's objective arms](../retail/simulation/mission-objectives.md#the-player-thinks-objective-arms)). For a finished order, **Route**, from the routes the subject's group's orders run (the group that places it, for an object), by name, with a link, then the group it is asked of and which of its orders it reads, the first on that route (`MissionIndex.SlotOnRoute`), or that it never comes true; a change to a finished order with no route takes the group's first order's route;
- **Status alert text**, its three lines, applied when a field lets go: "Shown in the status alert when the player presses Q, while this is the first win condition in the list not yet met, no lose condition holds and no enemy nearby is aware of the player; up to its first empty line." That is status 5, the only status whose body is an objective's text, which only Q raises ([`../retail/simulation/alert-panels.md`](../retail/simulation/alert-panels.md#its-text)). On a lose condition the fields are disabled, with "Never shown for a lose condition."; with no text, **Add status alert text** gives it three empty lines ([The mission's text](#the-missions-text));
- the campaign variables it sets the first time it comes true, up to ten ([Campaign variable writes](#campaign-variable-writes));
- Delete.

Row 17 has no GUID and nothing references an objective: the load renumbers no ref to one, and DBSIM's objective array (`004a9ec4`, its count `004a9ec0`) is referenced, by `es2_xref.py`, only in `DBSim_SpawnMissionObjects`, which fills it, and `Mission_EvaluateObjectives`, which walks it. An edit writes the record itself, a conditioned one included (retail's carry conditions 79 and 80). A mission file that ends inside its objectives (`DEMO2`) adds and deletes none.

Gaps:

- A subject change leaves a finished order's route as it was; the view then says it never comes true when the new subject's group runs no order on that route.
- A new objective goes last in row 17, so it is asked after every other, and nothing reorders them; the first win condition not yet met is the one whose status alert text the player reads.
- A later order's route is offered only once an objective reads it, and an objective offers only routes the group's orders already run, so an objective about a later order whose route differs from every earlier one's cannot be made from scratch.
- The condition ref, which the campaign layer authors, is not edited.

## The mission's text

The Mission Settings window (`MissionSettingsWindow`, its text drawn by `MissionTextView` through `MissionTextEditor`) lists the text package's objective lines (the F11 panel's, up to 10, of which the panel lists 7), then under "Campaign Only" the briefing lines and intelligence lines (up to 30 each; the mission screen shows them in a campaign, joined into one text). Each is a field with Up, Down and Remove, and Add a line after the last; the lists stay filled from their first slot, as block 13 assumes. A mission with no text package (`TRAIN1`, `TRAIN3`) gets one on its first line, as New mission writes one.

A line, here or in a status alert text, shows and writes the `.ENG` record that supplies its text, the last record of its id carrying the id's first record's condition ([Which record an edit writes](handoff-editor-document.md#which-record-an-edit-writes)). When another field references the same id (another objective, a text package slot, a trigger, a debrief slot, whatever its condition) the edited field first gets its own copy, which the view does not mention, since the edit changes only that field either way. A status alert text's ids are its id and the next two in the `.ENG`'s order. A removed line takes its id's records when nothing references the id any more. An add, or a shared line's copy, that would take the text past the 50 `mission.str` lines DBSIM reads is refused, with the reason under the button.

The view heads the lists with "Text used: N of 50 lines (briefing, objective lines, intelligence report, status alerts and debrief together).": `mission.str` holds every `.ENG` record the load keeps, whichever field references it, the debrief's lines included ([`../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md`](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-eng-string-table)). N counts one line per `.ENG` id, every version of the mission at once (`MissionTextEditing.Lines`), as the mission check does.

A field draws only the characters the game's fonts have glyphs for, codes 32 to 248; anything else typed shows in red and is left out when the field lets go, and the view notes "Accented letters show as spaces on the mission screen in the original 1.0 release.": version 1.0's mission screen shows a letter past code 127 as a space, and 1.10 keeps it ([`../retail/shell/mission-screen.md`](../retail/shell/mission-screen.md#the-summary-text-box)). Undo puts the `.ENG` back too. An objective's status alert text and the outliner's objective lines read the `.ENG` through the layout, as the view does.

### The debrief

The text is followed by **Debrief** (`DebriefView`, through `DebriefEditor`, read by `DebriefText`), noting "Shown on the mission screen after a campaign mission.", or for a mission the editor plays in training "Shown on the mission screen only after a campaign mission, so not after this one.", since a training debrief never reloads the mission's text ([`../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md`](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-debriefs-loss-line)). A mission with no row-5 record notes "This mission has no debrief." The view reads row 5's first record, the one the debrief keeps, and is laid out by outcome (the user's choice):

- **Outcome 1** on: each row-1 condition a replacement of a debrief line or a row-3 record of the debrief's movie carries, in the order the `.ENG` and then row 3 first use it, headed by its clauses as Present when words them, read at the debrief: "When the mission was won and campaign variable 250 is less than 1:". Flags 0, 8, 9 and 10 read as the mission just flown's ("the mission was won", "the number of squad HERCs written off after the mission"), since the debrief writes them before its reload ([`../retail/shell/campaign-loop.md`](../retail/shell/campaign-loop.md#the-debrief--game_processmissionresults-0040eae7)), and a roll is "of those debriefs" (`PresenceSentences.When`). A condition the clauses cannot read is named as the Conditions list names it. Under the heading each line: the outcome's own record of it as a field, else the Otherwise text dimmed, with "Dimmed lines read as under Otherwise."; then "Movie: N", the row-3 value, dimmed when the outcome has none of its own.
- **Otherwise** ("Text" when there is no outcome): "When no outcome holds:", each line's unconditioned text as a field, and the movie.
- **Report Hercs and squadmates lost**, a disabled checkbox, ticked when a debrief line carries the standard loss line: an unconditioned record and ten whose conditions read as the ten cases' clauses in order (`DebriefText.LossCases`), whatever their text. Its conditions are not outcomes and its line is not in their lists. "Its line comes first in the debrief." when it does; under **What it reports**, collapsed, "The last case that holds, and nothing when none does." and each case as "When the number of squadmates killed in the mission is 1:" with its text, read-only.

A field's edit writes the last `.ENG` record of the line's id carrying that outcome's condition, `-1` for Otherwise (`MissionTextEditing.SetLineUnder`), so the line's other records keep their text; a shared id is copied first, every record of it, and row 5's slot repointed at the copy (`MissionDocument.SetDebriefLine`, row 5 snapshotted whole for the undo), and a copy past the 50 lines is refused, as for every line.

Gaps:

- A text package line or a status alert text shows and edits its base text: a replacement under another condition is neither shown nor written, and an id with no `.ENG` record shows a note in place of its field.
- Only row 4's first record, which block 13 is written from, is edited; its briefing movie (`0x8E`) is not shown or set.
- The debrief's movie shows as its number and is not edited; outcomes are not added or removed and their clauses not edited; the loss line is not switched, its texts are not edited and its roll is not in the Random table view.
- Where two outcomes hold at once a line reads as the later record ([`../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md`](../retail/formats/msn-mission-file.md#the-eng-string-table)), as `C4_03`'s outcome 2 (won, with campaign variable 570 at 0) takes over outcome 1's (won); the view shows each outcome's own records and does not say which one wins.
- Nothing checks a line's length: the mission screen's wrap asserts on a word wider than its box ([`../retail/shell/mission-screen.md`](../retail/shell/mission-screen.md#the-summary-text-box)), and the status alert's and F11 panel's labels are not measured.
- A line's trailing newline, which ends a paragraph on the mission screen, is kept as the record had it and cannot be added or removed; a new line has none.
