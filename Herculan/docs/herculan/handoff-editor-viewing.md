# Handoff — opening and viewing a mission in the editor

Opening a mission, New mission, the theater and time of day, and how the editor shows and selects a mission's features. Part of the [editor handoff](handoff-editor.md).

## Opening a mission

The editor opens a mission only from its `.MSN`, never a `script.dat` such as `DATA\script.dat` or a save slot's `script%d.dat`, which have no `.MSN` to edit (the user's choice); `Herculan.Engine.Host` plays one, an exported one included ([What a saved `script.dat` reaches](handoff-editor-document.md#what-a-saved-scriptdat-reaches)). It starts with no mission and File > Load from VOL's list up, from the game's Editor menu item too. `Herculan.Engine.Host.Editor`'s second argument, after the install, names a mission as the list does (`C1_05` or `C1_05.MSN`, in any case, a saved or new one included); the list then says it is loading it and opens it as a pick from the list does, and a name no mission has leaves the list up with "No mission is named <name>." under it (`VolMissionBrowser.Find`).

File > Load from VOL lists every `.MSN` in the archives and every loose one in the install's `MSN\` folder, marking a loose one "(saved)" when it overrides an archive mission and "(new)" when no archive holds it. It opens one through `MissionOpener.Open` and `MissionFileLaunch.Open` (the simulator host's `.MSN` path), which writes the handoff into `%TEMP%\herculan-editor\`, so the install's `DATA\` is never written, and holds the mission as a `MissionDocument` whose generation draws what the handoff's load drew ([Conditions and variants](handoff-editor-document.md#conditions-and-variants)). The document's generated `script.dat` and `mission.str` are written over the handoff's, and the world is loaded for the mission generated from them with no object in it (`MissionScene.LoadWorld`), the one generation the editor makes before Export ([The generation](handoff-editor-document.md#the-generation)); the objects are then built for the records the document's layout places and stood on the ground as the game's load stands its own, footprints, pads and the one levelling pass included (`MissionScene.Settle`, [Rebuilding after an edit](handoff-editor-document.md#rebuilding-after-an-edit)). `Mission.SourcePath` is that scratch file.

## New mission

File > New mission (`NewMissionDialog`) asks for:

- a name, taken as Save As takes one, but never an archive mission's (the game would play it as that mission in its career position), asking first when the install has a saved mission of that name. A refused name shows its reason in strong red and tints the field;
- a zone, from every `dat\ZONE*.DAT` with its `.DBA` in DBSIM's mount (`MissionZones`), each with its size and the retail missions played in it: "Zone 502 (12.6 by 12.6 km): used by C5_02, C5_04", or "used by no retail mission". The missions are those the install's archives hold as they shipped (`ArchivedMissions`, the read a Cybrid's hardpoints share), read once per run, each played in the zone its row-2 base patches into header word 1 (`ZoneCatalog`); the Mission view names the open mission's zone the same way;
- a theater, named by the terrain bank its day descriptor loads (`TheaterNames`: `urban`, `bsnow`, `volcan`, `ice`, `moon`, [`../retail/formats/wld-world.md`](../retail/formats/wld-world.md)), since no retail text names one;
- day or night.

`NewMission` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/NewMission.cs`) builds the `.MSN` and `.ENG`: the player's HERC alone on a route of two points, its start and Waypoint 1, with one mandatory objective met when the player reaches the second, every record copied from `TRAIN1.MSN` but the text package, which takes `TRAIN5.MSN`'s shape; its comments cite each record. `MissionOpener.OpenNew` and `MissionFileLaunch.OpenNew` open it from memory as `INSTANT ACTION`, into the same scratch handoff. The title carries `*` until File > Save writes it (`EditHistory.MarkNeverSaved`); the browser then lists it "(new)". The mission check finds nothing in it.

## Theater and time of day

The outliner's first entry, Mission, shows `MissionView`: the name and zone (as [New mission](#new-mission) lists it), a Theater choice named as the New mission dialog names them, Day or Night, then the text ([The mission's text](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#the-missions-text)).

What retail plays ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#header-format)): the theater (header offset 0) picks `wld\world<theater * 2 + variant>`, the terrain texture bank, palette, sky and fog; the variant (offset 18) is day or night. Every retail load writes over the theater, a campaign's from its stage and a practice or `INSTANT ACTION` one as 1, and a practice or `INSTANT ACTION` load writes the practice screen's time of day over the variant; only a campaign mission plays its `.MSN`'s own day or night.

The editor shows a campaign mission as the game plays it, on its stage's theater (shown disabled, with a note) by its own day or night, and every other mission on its own theater and time of day, which the editor's open leaves to row 2 (`MissionOpener`). The planned menu for playing user-made missions is to honour them: a departure from `INSTANT ACTION` the user chose. The game's own launches still write theater 1 and the practice screen's time of day.

A change goes through `TheaterChanger` ([The tools](handoff-editor-document.md#the-tools)), and then dresses the scene in the new world (`MissionScene.ChangeTheater`): a model library in the new palette and shade ramp, every object's model and LOD chain built again (those of objects an undo can put back included), the terrain mesh lit again in the new bank, and the sky, fog, ground-shape set and beam colours. Heights, materials, objects, the selection, the camera and the view settings stay; `TerrainLeveller` keeps building rows against the scene's models as they stand. The host then frees every upload of the old world and uploads the scene again (`EditorHost.ReloadWorld`), and the scene is synced with the document as after any edit. A change costs about what an open costs, without loading the zone. A HERC's shadow already on the ground keeps its radius; every retail descriptor names the same ground-shape set (`FLAT2`). The view, the window title's zone and the scene's world after a change read the launch's header words (`MissionDocument.LaunchHeader`): row 2's base, with every word the launch writes, a campaign stage's theater, over it; a row-2 variation is not applied.

## Selection and panels

`EditorSelection` is an object, group, trigger area, trigger (a row-10 action), countdown (a row-11 timer), route (with an optional waypoint), objective or condition, held in one `SelectionState` set by the viewport click, the `MissionOutliner` (left: groups with members, areas, triggers, countdowns, routes, win and lose conditions, the objective lines the player reads, random choices, the conditions under a collapsed Advanced header, and the Problems list's findings) and the `PropertiesPanel` (right, one view per kind, every mentioned record a link).

Every kind names its `.MSN` record: a group, trigger area, trigger, countdown, route or condition by the GUID of its row (16, 9, 10, 11, 8, 1: `GroupSelection`, `AreaSelection`, `ActionSelection`, `TimerSelection`, `RouteSelection` with its waypoint, `ConditionSelection`), an objective by its index in row 17 (`ObjectiveSelection`), and an object by the row (12, 13 or 14, a squad herc's 12) and GUID of the roster record it stands for (`ObjectSelection`), which `MissionIndex` resolves to the scene's object. The editors take the same identities, and each selects the GUID or row-17 index it has just written. A selection is kept across every edit, undo and redo while the `.MSN` has its record ([Rebuilding after an edit](handoff-editor-document.md#rebuilding-after-an-edit)).

The scene's objects, groups, routes and trigger areas are the layout's (`MissionIndex`), every record included but a group arriving by drop pod or on foot ([Waiting groups](#waiting-groups)), and the picker, the selection boxes, the group overlay, the route and area overlay, the base pads, the mission box, the framing, the leveller and the move handles read them by GUID.

The outliner lists the layout too (`MissionLayout`, `MissionIndex`): every group with its members, and every trigger area, trigger, countdown, route and objective, a conditional one included, in layout order, each selecting its record by GUID or row-17 index. Each row is the record's name ([Names](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#names)), its number in its row from 1 (row 17's list for an objective) dimmed after it, cut to the panel's width with the whole name on hover: a group by its team, roster and members, with ", arrives when trigger 2 fires" for a group waiting on one; a member by its type, numbered "SCARAB 2 of 3" in member order when its group has more than one of that type, a squad HERC as the player's or a squadmate's; a trigger by what fires it and what it does; a countdown by its delay and what starts it; an area by its shape and who enters it; a route by its shape and who walks it. The objectives are listed as **Win and lose conditions**, each by its sentence, and the text package's objective lines apart, as **Objective list the player reads (F11)** ([Objectives](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#objectives)). An area whose corner or centre references a point the layout has none for, and a route none of whose waypoints does, stand nowhere and are named by what is missing ("Box with a corner missing", "Route with no waypoints"). Random choices lists every variant table in row-1 order by its title and how many records pick from it ([Variants](handoff-editor-conditions.md#variants)). A row whose record is not in every version of the mission, an object's by its group's condition too, ends "(not always present)", the words of the "Present when" field its view opens with.

Every view, the trigger, countdown, objective, object, group, route and trigger area views and a group's orders, reads the layout and `MissionIndex` by GUID or row-17 index, so it shows and edits any record of the `.MSN`, one the mission as opened leaves out included ([Triggers](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#triggers), [Countdowns](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#countdowns), [Objectives](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#objectives), [Orders](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#orders), [Groups](#groups), [Routes](handoff-editor-placement.md#routes), [Trigger areas](handoff-editor-placement.md#trigger-areas)), and so do a group's Waits on, an object's When engaged and When defeated ([Wiring](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#wiring)), a herc's chassis and hardpoints ([The player's squad](handoff-editor-roster.md#the-players-squad)) and the Toolbox's join, which takes the selected group by its GUID. The route and area views take their waypoints and corners from `MissionIndex`, which follows a drag's preview. A selection whose record the `.MSN` no longer has shows its kind and "It is no longer in the mission." until the sync after the edit clears it.

A number a designer sees counts from 1: a group, trigger area, trigger, countdown, route or objective by its place in its row ([Names](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#names)), in titles, links, pickers, outliner rows, the scene's labels and the overlays, delete blockers and the Problems list; orders, hardpoints and squadmates by their place in their list; a route's waypoints as the cockpit numbers them, after the start ([Routes](handoff-editor-placement.md#routes)). `MissionText.Number` is the one place a record's number is computed from its place, by the user's decision ([`handoff-editor-ux-plan.md`](handoff-editor-ux-plan.md#decided)); HercWorks.Query and the docs that cite a file's records keep their indices.

## Camera

`EditorCamera` flies free (right mouse to look, WASD/arrows, Q/E, Shift). A double-click in the viewport or the outliner selects and frames the selection (`SelectionBounds`, `EditorCamera.Frame`), keeping the yaw and looking down on it. A double-click on a link in the Properties panel selects without framing, and a countdown, a group out of the scene and its members are never framed.

V, or View > Top-down, looks down from above, north up, high enough over the middle of the zone that its whole grid fits the window; looking turns about the vertical alone, W is up the screen, fog is off and the clip planes are pushed out with the height. V again returns to the pose it left; opening another mission leaves it. It is the perspective camera at its pitch limit, 88 degrees down, not an orthographic map: things stand up toward the edges and a point's place on screen shifts with its height. It frames the grid as it opens, not after a resize.

How a structure's ground plane (shadow, floor plates) draws from that height is not looked at: it is ranked against the terrain by the cell `HeightGrid.PickDrawCell` files it under in the visible region, which `TerrainVisibleRegion.Update` keeps from the last view whenever the frustum, cut off at the zone's visibility range, reaches no ground.

## Picking

A press on a move handle of the selection, a selected trigger area's corners and radius included, is taken in screen space first (`MoveGizmo`). Otherwise `ScenePicker` tries a waypoint in screen space, then an object (skipping a group out of the scene, and a waiting group when View > Waiting Groups hides it): the bounding spheres the ray meets give the candidates, and the nearest triangle wins, a structure's ground plane counting only when the ray meets nothing solid, so a Herc on a base's pad is picked rather than the base; then `GroundPick` (a ray march against `HeightAtWorld`) and the smallest trigger area containing that point.

## Overlays

`MissionOverlay` draws the layout's features (`MissionIndex`) through `DrapedLines`, each toggled from the View menu, a line behind terrain dimmed:

- every trigger area of row 9 whose points the layout has, coloured by who trips it (the first trigger testing it), grey when no trigger tests it, labelled "Area 3" by its number;
- every route of row 8 with a waypoint the layout has, in three colours (the player's, walked, never walked), loops closed, labelled "Route 2" by its number, and when selected its waypoints "Start", "1", "2" as the route view names them;
- every base pad's painted tile (`MissionBasePad.TileOrigin`), with the selected group's authored point and its snap;
- the play area (View > Play Area), the mission box, the extent of every row-6 point, with its HDD-map and abort margins.

The numbers are the ones the outliner and every view show ([Selection and panels](#selection-and-panels)).

A selected area's ground is tinted yellow over exactly what its test catches, a circle's octagon included, through `SceneRenderer.Highlight`, which only the editor's program compiles. Only a selected area is tinted; a selected trigger's areas are outlined, since `Highlight` holds one region. The tint covers the terrain, not a structure's ground plane inside the area.

## Cross-references

`MissionIndex` works out from the layout which triggers test each area (a trigger's slots up to the first unset one) and which groups walk or merely name each route, and for each trigger the countdowns and objects that fire it and the groups waiting on it, the order slots it ends and the countdowns it starts (`TriggerWiring`). The trigger view shows all of it with the campaign variables it sets and its message, and an area's view shows it read-only for every trigger testing the area (`TriggerView.DrawSummary`). It also works out the objectives about each group and object, the group whose orders an objective's finished order reads, which of that group's orders it reads, and so which later orders an objective reads (`MissionIndex.ObjectivesReadingOrder`), and the group slots running each order (`MissionIndex.OrderSlots`).

## Groups

A group's setup is its point (with its pre-snap authored point and where it came from), heading (and whether it is the route bearing), formation id and paints-ground flag (`MissionGroupSetup`), the layout's, for the scene and the group view alike (`MissionIndex`). Selecting a group boxes its members and highlights the route it walks; `GroupOverlay` draws, from the layout, a post at its point, a heading arrow, and a line to each order's target in the scene labelled with the order ("1st order: Patrol"). A group out of the scene gets only its route's highlight. Selecting an objective boxes its subject, or its subject group's members.

The group view (`GroupView`) shows, in order: its name, Present when and Team; for the player's squad, its machines; the trigger it waits on; where it starts; its heading and base pad; its members, as links; the campaign variables it sets when every member is destroyed or immobilized ([Campaign variable writes](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#campaign-variable-writes)); the win and lose conditions about it; its orders; and, collapsed under Advanced, its formation and its own route field.

- **Starts at** says where its members spread from ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#placement--the-actual-rule), rules 2 and 3): "its own position", or "the start of its route" with a link to the route its first order names. A group with neither stands at the map's corner (0, 0) in the editor (`MissionPlacementRules.GroupSetup`), and the view says to give it a position or a route and that where it starts in play is not known: no retail doc says what DBSIM does with such a group. The fields under it move it as its move handles do, and what else stands on its point is listed ([Moving](handoff-editor-placement.md#moving)).
- **Base pad**, for a structure group whose record sets the paints-ground flag and whose formation has a pad ([`../retail/formats/script-dat.md`](../retail/formats/script-dat.md#base-formation-terrain)): it lays its concrete pad on the ground tile under its position, the tile's size in world units and metres, with a note that its structures stand at a fixed spot in that tile, so a move within it changes nothing and one into the next tile jumps a tile, and that the ground under the pad is levelled. Otherwise "Base pad: none", with why when the flag is set (no structure, or a formation with no pad, which paints nothing). A herc or flyer group whose flag is set gets a note that it has no effect in play.
- **Advanced**: the formation, "Formation: 3 of dat\MFORMS.DAT" (`FFORMS.DAT` for flyers, `BFORMS.DAT` for structures), with a note that formations have no names, since no retail file names one; and "Own route:", the group record's route field (row 16 `0x36`), as a link or none, with a note that it is not editable yet, that the group walks its first order's route, and that what reads the field in play is not known.

The object view (`ObjectView`) shows its name ("SCARAB"; a structure or ground vehicle by its kind and the name the MFD's F5 screen prints, "Structure: SUPPLY DEPOT", "Ground vehicle:" before a ground vehicle's, or "Structure with no name on the MFD") and its group, Present when and the random choice it picks from ([Variants](handoff-editor-conditions.md#variants)); its Team, fixed; its type and fit ([Type and fit](handoff-editor-roster.md#type-and-fit)); its position as fields that move it, and its heading; its starting condition, or for a machine of the player's squad which Herc it is ([The player's squad](handoff-editor-roster.md#the-players-squad)); the triggers it fires when engaged and when defeated ([Wiring](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#wiring)); the campaign variables it sets when put out of action; the win and lose conditions about it; Delete, or what refuses it ([Deleting](handoff-editor-roster.md#deleting)); and, collapsed under Advanced, its type number, model, triangle and texture counts and hit radius.

Not drawn:

- ghosts of the formation slots a group leaves empty;
- an order's own point (`+0x04`, set in 47 orders), which only the briefing map reads, off the squad's first order ([`../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md`](../retail/simulation/ai-goals.md#the-order-record)): a briefing-only marker, or nothing.

## Waiting groups

A group waiting on a trigger whose arrival brings it in by drop pod or on foot (verbs 2 to 5) is not in the scene (`PlannedGroup.OffScene`), by the user's choice: its members are neither drawn nor picked and get no box, label, order line, framing or move handle, as retail neither draws nor touches such a group before it arrives and never uses its point, bringing it in about the player when the trigger fires ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#arrival--group_deploymentcheck-004236c4)). Its route is still drawn. Its members are still built and indexed and are selected from the outliner, and the group's view and each member's say it is not in the scene and how it arrives. A structure group stays in the scene whatever its action's verb, as DBSIM builds a held-back structure where it is placed, footprint and pad included ([`../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md`](../retail/simulation/mission-deployment.md#held-back-structures-stand-from-the-start)).

View > Waiting Groups draws, outlines (meshes hidden, members boxed, still selectable) or hides the members of every other group gated on a trigger: one arriving in place, or a structure group. Unless hidden, each is labelled at its point with the trigger and the arrival ("Group 5: waits on trigger 2, in place"), labels sharing a point stacked; the label numbers the group and the trigger as the outliner numbers them. Translucent ghosts in place of outlines need a tint or alpha path in `SceneRenderer`.

## Mission check

The mission check finds what makes a mission misbehave in play: references to records it does not have, records the game never reads, shapes it crashes on, and rosters and text past what it holds. `MissionCheck` (Core, `Data/File/Msn/MissionCheck.cs`) runs over the layout, every version of the mission at once: each thing of rows 6 to 16 as its base, then as each record of its GUID under another condition sets it over the base (the record whole for a row that replaces), then with each option of a random choice its records draw, other than the one the layout shows, copied in; a problem only those versions have is marked so. It checks the condition refs of rows 1, 2 and 17 and of every `.ENG` line too. A reference is missing when no record of the row it names carries the GUID at all. Group 0 stands for the squad, which DBSIM builds from `player.mec`, so the rules about a herc group's first order and a group's kind are not applied to it; the rest are. Each finding names its `.MSN` record (row and GUID, row-17 index or `.ENG` id) and carries its kind (`MissionCheckKind`), whose DBSIM reasons are `ScriptDatLint`'s (`ScriptLintKind`).

The editor runs it at the open and after every edit, undo and redo, moves and side changes included, and lists the findings under Problems, each selecting the record it names (`MissionLint`): a condition, route, area, trigger, countdown or group by its GUID, an objective by its row-17 index, a herc, flyer or structure as its object in the scene, and an order as the first group, in layout order, whose orders reference it. A finding on a point, a heading, the header, a text line, a roster as a whole or a roster record no group places selects nothing. Two missing references of one record to the same row are listed once. Save checks the same and lists the same sentences ([Saving and export](handoff-editor-document.md#saving-and-export)). The Problems list says each finding in the editor's words by its kind (`ProblemText`): the record by the name and number every view gives it, with "(in some versions)" after it when only some versions have the problem, what goes wrong in play, then what to change, a control by its label. The check's own message, in `.MSN` terms, is the HercWorks tools'.

| Kind | The check says | The Problems list says |

Add one with "Add..." > "Set a campaign variable", or remove the message." |

The rosters are counted as the adds count them, every GUID of the row whatever its condition and, for HERCs, one machine per squad position ([Joining and caps](handoff-editor-roster.md#joining-and-caps)); the text as the text budget counts it, one line per `.ENG` id ([The mission's text](handoff-editor-mission-logic.md#the-missions-text)).

`HercWorks.Query lint` runs the check over every retail mission; `lint --generated` runs `ScriptDatLint` instead, `MissionScriptForm`'s ref-range checks and the same rules in `script.dat` terms, over the `script.dat` each mission's load writes with every campaign variable 0 and every draw 0, one version per mission, the block-7 cap counted against the roster alone. Each finds one thing: in `C1_02`, the trigger whose message is `MAYDAY!`, which writes no campaign variable ([`../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md`](../retail/simulation/cockpit-messages.md#its-speakerless-set)).

## Not shown

- **Drop-pod and on-foot arrivals**: such a group is not in the scene, since it arrives at a point picked from the player's position and heading when the trigger fires ([Waiting groups](#waiting-groups)).
- **Detection and engagement ranges** (50,000 and 100,000 units), simulation constants rather than mission data.
- A **trigger graph**: the trigger view walks the wiring one link at a time, where a node graph of triggers, countdowns and the objects and groups between them would show it at once.

## Open

- **Open:** the mission check does not check a reference to a record present only under other conditions than the referrer's: a reference counts as missing only when no record of its row carries the GUID at all.
- **Open:** an object's view names only the group that places it; a later group of another condition that references the same record is not listed there, though the user's choice is that it is ([Rebuilding after an edit](handoff-editor-document.md#rebuilding-after-an-edit)).
