# Plan — ES1's first playable mission

**The first playable slice of *Metaltech: Earthsiege*: a mission loaded in the ES1 zone viewer with the player's HERC under the player's control — walking, aiming, targeting and firing from ES1's cockpit, on ES1's tick — against enemy HERCs that take damage, and then run ES1's AI.** The player's mechanics come first and the AI last, so each player mechanic can be checked against retail on its own before anything else moves. The work is port first: the ES1 docs already hold the HERC ([`mech.md`](../es1/simulation/mech.md)), the frame loop and tick ([`main-loop.md`](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md)), the input ([`input.md`](../es1/simulation/input.md)), the cockpit ([`cockpit-views.md`](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md), [`cockpit-panel.md`](../es1/simulation/cockpit-panel.md)) and the AI ([`mech-ai.md`](../es1/simulation/mech-ai.md)), and reverse-engineering is limited to the gaps a port step meets, read as a targeted batch before that step. This plan carries out [phase 2](plan-es1-support.md#phase-2--the-engine) of the ES1 plan for the simulation.

## Rules

- Behaviour is ported from what the binary shows. Nothing is invented to reach a playable build sooner; where the slice lacks a retail feature, the code does nothing in its place, tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` and listed under [what the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out) until its step lands.
- No shortcut that a later step has to undo: a structure retail builds (the weapon mounts, the view's window) is built as retail builds it, even when the step that uses it fully comes later.
- The ES1 docs describe ES1; the port's types and departures go in doc comments on the C#, citing the doc section.

### What the slice leaves out

Until its step lands, each of these does nothing, tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` in code:

- **The hit flash**, no step yet: the impact palette (`dpl\IMPACT1.DPL`) is not loaded, so a hit shakes the view without the palette's flicker ([the hit shake](../es1/simulation/cockpit-panel.md#the-hit-shake)); and the effects' billboards, the sky's bands, the skyline and the clouds keep the colors of the palette they were built in, where retail draws them as indices through the live palette ([installing colors](../es1/rendering/palette.md#installing-colors)), so neither that flicker nor the death flash reaches them (decision 18).
- **Sound**, until item 7's modules B–E land: the simulator's effects play through decision 26's stand-in for the digital driver; the rest play nothing: the adaptive music (`Music_Tick`, [the music](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md#the-music--music_tick-000585dc)), the pilots' voice lines, and the shell's songs, its buttons' click, its talking heads' lines and the `.TTM` scripts' sounds. Their latches and state run, and a talking pilot's face still follows its script.
- **The joystick's calibration**, no step yet: the calibration panel (`00049a7c` and its neighbours, [`joystick.md#open`](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#open)) and `data\joy1.cal`/`joy2.cal` are not read; a modern controller gives calibrated values in their place (decision 9). The controls panel's CALIBRATE rebuilds the devices and raises no panel.
- **A modern controller's hat**, until a retail check settles which hat byte is which direction ([`joystick.md#open`](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#open)): the hat is not read, so the Thrustmaster FCS's hat line (port axis 3) carries whatever the throttle role holds, and the FlightstickPro's hat comes only from its buttons held together, as retail decodes them.
- **`data\mem.got`**, no step: `Sim_Main`'s write of the heap's size ([starting](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md#starting--sim_main-00059350)) is not made, since this engine has no fixed heap to give the size of.
- **A cockpit scheme above 7**, no step yet: its `0x40` word, which retail reads past its table ([the color scheme](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#the-color-scheme)), keeps the second range's colour at `0x40`.

## Decisions

Made with the user:

1. **The animation classes are the core's.** `ShapeAnimation`, `AnimationThread`, `ShapeInstance` and `SkeletonPose` are in `Herculan.Engine.Anim`, since the 3Space classes belong to the core ([how the two games share code](planning.md#how-the-two-games-share-code)). ES1's are ES2's but for one rounding: a seek scales the position with ES1's rounding Q14 multiply ([differences](../es1/rendering/dts-node-posing.md#differences-from-es2)). The caller of a seek names its arithmetic (`SeekRounding`), so neither game's behaviour changes and the core tests no game.
2. **The tick is paced as retail paces it.** ES1 runs one tick a frame, its length the time the frame took (`SimTickDelta`, Q8 with 1.0 = 100 ms, held to 25–176 ms), and a frame waits at least two PC timer ticks ([the tick length](../es1/simulation/fixed-point-math.md#the-tick-length--time_beginsimtick-00063f40)). The timer runs at 120 interrupts a second ([the PC timer](../es1/simulation/timer.md#the-rate)), so retail's minimum frame time is two of them, 16.7 ms, which the tick's floor counts as 25 ms: a machine that draws a frame that fast plays at 1.5 times real time, where the hardware of its day took 25 ms or more a frame and played in real time. Every frame past a view change's cooldown also ends waiting for the monitor's next vertical retrace, 70 a second ([the view command](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#cockpitview_processviewcommand-000226f0)), a wait that ends before the next tick is due on a machine that keeps up, and so does not slow it. An ES1 pacer runs a tick once **three** interrupts' time, 25 ms, has passed, with the elapsed time counted in 120 Hz interrupts as the tick's length under retail's clamp and arithmetic, holds the frame's end to the next retrace of a 70 Hz monitor as retail does, and shows the display every host frame, as retail draws no frame between ticks. The three-interrupt wait is this engine's departure from retail's two, the user's choice, so the game plays in real time as it did on period hardware; it is tagged in code and listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
3. **The view is view 0 as retail builds it.** The world is drawn in the window and with the projection of the chassis's `.VUE` record, clipped by its clip regions, under the canopy art from `db0\<chassis>` ([the view manager](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#the-view-manager), [the canopy art](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#loading-and-freeing-the-canopy-art)), from the eye-point camera `Cam_AttachTo` attaches. The viewer's free camera stays on a key, as an engine aid with no retail counterpart.
4. **The sensor model is ported** from [`sensor-model.md`](../es1/simulation/sensor-model.md): the AI's target choice reads the contacts and radar flags `Sim_DetectionTick` sets.
5. **The player's mechanics come before the AI**: movement, the joystick, the cockpit, then weapons and damage, so each is checked against retail on its own; the AI is the last step.
6. **A named mission loads as a single mission, and the simulator runs on the install's `data\` files.** The shell's mode-0 load writes `data\script.dat`, `data\mission.var` and `data\player.mec` into the install, and the simulator reads whatever those files hold, as `DBSIM.EXE` does, so it also runs on a set retail's shell wrote. A career's missions load through the shell's mode 1 ([a career's next mission](../es1/shell/career.md#a-careers-next-mission)). `--theater` and `--world` override header words 0, 8 and 9, as engine aids.
7. **The 320×200 screen is shown at 4:3**, as a CRT showed it, with the `.VUE` projection in the 320×200 screen's pixels.
8. **A textured face is filled as retail fills it**, row by row with the texture interpolated in screen space, the walk the scene's clip mode 2 selects ([bitmap fills](../es1/rendering/polygon-shading.md#bitmap-fills-brush-modes-5-and-7), [the display drivers](../es1/rendering/polygon-shading.md#the-display-drivers)), the walk run in the fragment shader as the clouds' is. The perspective-correct mapping the core's renderer already does is the "Perspective-correct textures" tweak, off by default.
9. **A modern controller enters at the calibrated-axis level.** Retail times the game port's axis lines in port reads, a count whose worth depends on the machine, and maps it through a calibration the player records ([the joystick library](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#normalising-an-axis)). The port cannot exist on modern hardware, so each of its four axes arrives as the value a calibrated stick gives, −128 to +128, and everything after it is retail's: the devices, their response, the class readers, the poll and the merge ([the joystick devices](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#the-joystick-devices)). The controller's roles are wired onto the port as its own wiring: stick X and Y on axes 0 and 1, the rudder on axis 2, the throttle on axis 3. The calibration panel and the `.cal` files stay [left out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out).
10. **The stick model comes from preferences option 7**, as retail's: `data\prefs.cfg`'s, changed on the controls panel's JOYSTICK row, which rebuilds the devices when the panel closes ([the controls panel](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#the-controls-panel--controlspanel_ctor-00048408)).
11. **A panel runs inside the tick, as retail's does.** Retail raises a panel from the tick's commands and runs the panel's own loop there until it closes, then finishes the tick with its answer ([the loop](../es1/simulation/alert-panels.md#the-loop), [`PreferencesPanel_Raise`](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#raising-the-panel--preferencespanel_raise-00057820)). The port suspends the tick where retail enters the loop and runs one pass of the loop a host frame, the PC timer running on, where retail's loop runs with no wait or, for the preferences panel, once a retrace; the tick goes on from there when the panel closes, as the frame's end goes on after a wait for the monitor (decision 2).
12. **`Ctrl+Q`'s and `Ctrl+Esc`'s QUIT ends the mission**, which closes the viewer, as retail's quit flag ends the simulator; `Q` puts the mission's status up in the status panel, as retail's does ([commands](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md#commands--sim_dispatchcommand-00059df8)).
13. **The preferences panel's DONE always saves options 0–6.** Retail's saves them only when one differs from its load-time copy, which the controls panel's DONE makes current, so a change made before CONTROLS is opened and closed is lost at the next mission ([saving](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#saving)). The user chose the fix; it is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
14. **A stick the controls panel builds no device for is left with none**, as the code means to: choosing `NONE` leaves no stick enabled, and a model that needs one stick leaves the second slot empty. Retail's `ControlsPanel_ApplyDevices` enables the device it has just freed in each case ([rebuilding the joystick devices](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#rebuilding-the-joystick-devices--controlspanel_applydevices-00048e98), whose Open asks what that device then reads); the departure is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
15. **The shell's sound manager counts as present** until item 7's module C ports it, as on a retail machine with a sound card: start-up leaves options 0–2 as `data\prefs.cfg` holds them, so MISSION PARAMS' ACCEPT and CANCEL write them back unchanged, and what plays through the manager plays nothing (the sound line of [what the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out)). The simulator's manager (`Sound_Manager`, `000959ac`, [the preferences panel's loop](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#the-loop--preferencespanel_run-00047a88)) is the one `resource.cfg` makes (decision 25), so the preferences panel's MUSIC, SOUNDS and RADIO and the mission start's reset of options 0–2 with no manager follow it.
16. **A click on the gunsight picks the object drawn at the clicked pixel in this engine's draw.** Retail's pixel pick ([the pick](../es1/simulation/terrain.md#the-pixel-pick-and-the-occlusion-probe)) keeps the last object its farthest-first painter's draw paints over the point; this engine draws the shapes and HERCs with a depth test, so the pick reads back which object the GPU drew at the point, the one the player sees under the pointer. The two differ only where retail's sort paints a farther object over a nearer one. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` with the depth test.
17. **The MFD's NAV MAP and missile camera draw the world with the GPU, into the MFD's screen rect.** Retail draws the NAV MAP's zone shapes and the missile camera's scene with its software rasterizer, in palette indices, into the MFD's rect ([the NAV MAP's paint](../es1/simulation/mfd-nav-map.md#the-paint), [the missile camera](../es1/simulation/mfd-missile-camera.md)). This engine draws them as it draws view 0's world, in a world pass into the rect at the window's resolution, and marks as world only the cells the pass covers, so the NAV MAP's fill and cross, and whatever else the 2D drawing leaves there, stay palette indices shown through the live palette. The shapes are sharper than retail's and drawn with this engine's depth test (decision 16). The user's choice.
18. **The world's colors follow the live palette.** Retail's whole screen is palette indices shown through the one live palette, so whatever is installed into it shows on the world as on the cockpit ([installing colors](../es1/rendering/palette.md#installing-colors)). This engine draws the world on the GPU in full color, resolving its indices through tables built from the palette (decisions 8 and 17), so whenever the live palette changes it rebuilds the world's palette-derived tables from it: the shaded faces' ramps, the table the textured and solid faces read and the ground. The death flash so reaches the faces and the ground as well as the cockpit art. The effects' billboards, the sky's bands, the skyline and the clouds keep the colors they were built in ([what the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out), the hit flash). The user's choice.
19. **The Heads-Down Display's command screen draws its map's ground as decision 17's NAV MAP does.** Retail draws the zone's shapes seen from above with its software rasterizer, every lit face in palette entry `0x38` through ramp 14 ([drawing the map](../es1/simulation/hdd-command-screen.md#drawing-the-map)); this engine draws them in a world pass into the map viewport with that ramp pointed at the one entry, and the grid, the border, the markers and the chosen unit's frame stay palette indices drawn over it. The user's choice.
20. **A tape plays at the pace it was recorded.** Retail's playback skips `Time_BeginSimTick` and gives each frame the tape's recorded `SimTickDelta` ([recording and playback](../es1/simulation/input.md#recording-and-playback)), so its frames come as fast as the machine draws them, which on a modern machine is the 70 Hz retrace the frame's end waits for (decision 2), where the retail tapes were recorded at 25 to 176 ms a frame. The port holds each playback frame for its recorded delta, as ES2's `--play` does ([playback](input-tapes.md#playback)), so a demo plays in the time it was recorded in, as live play does under decision 2. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
21. **`-d` is not taken**, as ES2's engine does not take it. `es2_xref.py` finds no caller of `Vcr_Checkpoint`, the routine that fills the checkpoint file ([the checkpoint file](../es1/formats/tap-input-tape.md#the-checkpoint-file)), so what retail's `-d` is known to do is make an empty `<stem>.dmp` while recording and hold it open in playback. The user's choice; listed in `ROADMAP.md`.
22. **The shell's typed characters are the host's typed text.** Retail's shell lets the PC BIOS have every key its keyboard subscribers do not claim, and reads the characters the BIOS's own US table makes of them through DOS ([the input devices](../es1/simulation/input.md#the-input-devices)), code that is not in `GO.EXE`. This engine stands a key buffer in for the BIOS's, each key's character the one the host types for it, so the REGISTRATION window's name box takes letters as the player's keyboard layout gives them. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
23. **SYSTEM REPAIR's PROCEED keeps retail's free level.** Once salvage was spent in the window, retail's PROCEED sets the selected item to `Repair_TargetForLevel` of the level the STATUS list shows, which after a + is the level above the one + paid for, so the item rises one level further at no cost ([CANCEL and PROCEED](../es1/shell/repair-and-build.md#cancel-and-proceed)). The port does the same; the "No free repair level" tweak, off by default, makes PROCEED set no status, keeping only what + and − paid for. The user's choice.
24. **A pass of the lift's ride takes three PC timer interrupts' drawing time.** Each pass of `LiftStart_Rise` ends in `Vga_WaitVerticalRetrace`, which ends at once while a retrace is in progress ([the ride](../es1/simulation/lift-start.md#the-ride--liftstart_rise-00057ffc)), so on retail's machines a pass took as long as it took to draw, rounded up to a retrace. The port draws in no time, so each pass stands 25 ms, decision 2's frame minimum, before its frame's end, and the 115 passes take about 3.3 s. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
25. **The sound devices are the install's `resource.cfg`'s**, as retail's setup writes them ([the driver](../es1/formats/sound-samples.md#the-driver)): a `[SoundFX]` `Driver#` turns the digital sounds on, and a `[Music]` `Driver#` plays the songs through the FM driver, routed as retail routes a song's tracks for `A002` ([the devices](../es1/formats/hmp-music.md#the-devices)); a blank `Driver#` makes no sound manager, as retail's does. Only the sound the music was written for is ported: the FM driver and device `0xa005`. A `Driver#` naming another MIDI device (Sound Master II, MPU 401, MT-32, AWE 32) plays FM too, where retail's would route the songs to that device; the departure is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` when module D lands, and those devices are [Open](#open). The user's choice.
26. **Until HMI's digital driver is read (item 7's module E), a sound's volume reaches the mixer linearly and every sound plays in the center**: the gain is the start block's volume over `0x7fff`, the pan the center, as retail's sounds play on the user's mono `e015` device, and the host's mixer resamples the 11,025 Hz data. Each is tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` and replaced by the driver's own curves in module E. The user's choice.
27. **The FM driver is read with the music (module D)**, not with the other HMI driver internals: the songs are written for it ([the devices](../es1/formats/hmp-music.md#the-devices)), so no rendering of them matches retail's without it. The digital driver's mixing stays last (module E). The user's choice; the OPL emulator is chosen when module D starts ([Open](#open)).
28. **The flyers' shared sound plays from the first flyer to move each frame, as retail's does.** Every flyer offers its position for catalog row 16, and retail's `Sound_OfferPosition` keeps the first point a frame offers, its test of a kept distance comparing the dword with itself ([placed sounds](../es1/formats/sound-samples.md#placed-sounds)). The port does the same; the "Hear the nearest flyer" tweak, off by default, keeps the nearest point instead. The user's choice.
29. **Placed sounds play in stereo, panned across the camera's view.** Retail's placement pans a sound by its offset along the world's x axis, whatever the camera's heading, rounds a sound within 1,024 units of the camera to a whole axis, and reaches only a voice as it starts ([placed sounds](../es1/formats/sound-samples.md#placed-sounds)); on the user's mono `e015` device every sound plays in the center (decision 26). The "Stereo sound" tweak, on by default, pans each placed sound by where it lies across the camera's view, every frame and on a voice already playing; off, every sound plays in the center. Retail's placement still sets the volume either way. This engine's invention, the user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.

## Steps

Each module is split into parts with the user before it starts and taken to [done](plan-es1-support.md#when-a-module-is-done) before the next: RE batches, each an [ES1 batch](handoff-es1-data-formats.md#how-the-batches-were-run) limited to what the next port part needs and written up in the ES1 docs before the port reads them, then the port parts, one doc batch over their details, and the user's check against retail where retail can be seen. A module's positive control is the retail check in the Open of the docs it wrote.

The slice is done: steps 0–8 (the animation seam, the HERC's body, the mission start and the single-mission load, the mission's machines and the forward view, the tick and the player's movement, the joystick, the cockpit, weapons and damage, the AI). After it, by the module rows of [step 0.7](plan-es1-support.md#phase-0--tooling-and-measurement):

1. Flyers. Done ([the flyers](../es1/simulation/flyers.md)).
2. Effects and structures. Done ([the structures](../es1/simulation/structures.md)); the lift a friendly structure starts is item 6.
3. The mission's end. Done ([the mission status](../es1/simulation/mission-status.md), [the main loop](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md)).
4. The rest of the cockpit, the Heads-Down Display's two pages. Done ([the command screen](../es1/simulation/hdd-command-screen.md), [the damage screen](../es1/simulation/hdd-damage-screen.md)). The calibration panel, `cal_alrt.cpp`, stays [left out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out) (decision 9); its code, the unreferenced cluster `0004571c` before `alert.cpp` and the four small functions among the `Prefs_*` helpers are read with the library region.
5. `vcr.cpp`, `memory.cpp` and the rest of the library region ([`handoff-es1-library-region.md`](handoff-es1-library-region.md)); then the shell. The tapes ([the tape page](../es1/formats/tap-input-tape.md)) and `memory.cpp` are done; the library region is paused, its functions read as the port parts need them. The shell is done in five modules in the order a player meets them: S1 the frame and the main menu, S2 a single mission end to end, S3 the career, S4 the hangar, S5 the war map, then the options screens 1310 and 1320 ([the shell's pages](../es1/shell/main-menu.md)).
6. The lift: `LiftStart_DarkenPalette` (`00057cf4`) and `LiftStart_Rise` (`00057ffc`), with the pitch gradient its darkening runs ([the pitch gradient](../es1/rendering/ground-and-sky.md#the-pitch-gradient-palette-entries-6479)). Done ([the lift start](../es1/simulation/lift-start.md)).
7. Sound, in five modules in this order, each split into parts with the user before it starts and taken to done before the next. The simulator's and the shell's sound managers are one code ([the sound manager](../es1/formats/sound-samples.md#the-sound-manager)), so they are one port over the core's `IAudioBackend`, with the devices by decision 25.
   - **A — the effects.** RE 136: the sound file's unnamed functions (`0004e294`, `0004e2c8` and `0004e2f9`, the options' handlers; `0004fb60` and `0004fdd8`, the listener's frame and what it sets on each placed sound; the callbacks `0004e718`, `0004e7b4` and `0004e7fc`; the object base `00050020`, `000500d8`, `00050164`; the vtables' slots `+0x28`, `+0x2c`, `+0x30`; the manager's limits `0004ffe0`, `00050008`), `SOUNDS.STR`'s attribute bytes, and where `Sim_StartMission` takes `Sound_Init`'s six values from. Port: the manager, its sound objects and `DigiSound`, the catalog, `Sound_Play`, `Sound_PlayAt`, `Sound_Stop`, `Sound_IsPlaying`, the listener and the options' handlers (decision 26), and the simulator's effect placeholders. RE 136, the parts A1a (the manager and its objects), A1b (`Sound_Init`, the catalog, the host's audio, the click), A2 (placed sounds) and A3 (the remaining simulator call sites), and doc batch 137 are done; the module's retail check is next.
   - **B — the voice lines.** `SpeechLine_Load`'s sample, `Snc_Start`'s play and `Sound_Init` with no manager ([`snc-lip-sync.md`](../es1/formats/snc-lip-sync.md#playing-a-line)); the face keeps the script's time, not the recording's. The part B1 and doc batch 138 are done; the module's retail check is next.
   - **C — the shell's sound.** `ShellSound_Init` with `ES1GO 00041f64` and `0004240a`, the 20 names, the buttons' click `ES1GO 0007fd00`, the talking heads' `.RAW` lines (`ShellSnc_OpenSound`), the `.TTM` scripts' sound token, and the songs' cues (`ShellMusic_Play`, the MUSIC box), whose objects play nothing until D. The `.ANM` sounds wait for the shell's `.ANM` player. Parts: RE 139, C1 (the manager and the clicks), C2 (the talking heads' lines and the scripts' sounds), C3 (the songs' cues), then doc batch 140.
   - **D — the music.** First the tooling the drivers need ([Open](#open)), then the HMI library's song player in the image (the events after a track's first delta, the tempo on the HMI timer, the branch table, the volume, `MidiSound_Play`'s callees and callbacks), the routing, device `0xa005` with `sos\drum32.dig`, the FM driver `fmmidi.com` from `SOS\HMIMDRV.386` (decision 27); `Music_Tick` and the shell's songs.
   - **E — the digital driver.** The mix of the digital driver `resource.cfg` names, from `SOS\HMIDRV.386`: the rate, the volume and pan curves, the 8-bit data, the 32 voices; it replaces decision 26's placeholders.

## Retail checks

Each is cheap to see and settles something a step rests on:

- **The PC timer's rate**: breakpoints on `Pit_SetDivisor` (`0006d18c`) and `Time_InitSimClock` (`00063e64`) under DOSBox-X confirm the divisor 9,939 and the 120 interrupts a second read from the code ([the PC timer](../es1/simulation/timer.md#open)).
- **Walking speed**: the time a HERC of one chassis takes at full throttle between two of a zone's placed shapes, against the port.
- **Starting positions**: the player's and an enemy group's position at a mission's start, against the port run on the three `data\` files retail's shell wrote for that start.

## Open

- **Open:** every placeholder [the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out), each an **Unported:** item in `ROADMAP.md` once its step lands.
- **Open:** the OPL emulator the FM driver's port drives (item 7's module D, decision 27), chosen with the user when D starts; the project's MIT license bounds the choice.
- **Open:** the MIDI devices other than FM (decision 25): the tracks listing `0xa000`, which Sound Master II, MPU 401 and AWE 32 play, through a General MIDI synthesizer or a MIDI output to the player's own device, and MT-32, which plays only the `0xa005` tracks.
- **Open:** the tooling for item 7's modules D and E: `SOS\HMIMDRV.386` and `SOS\HMIDRV.386` are containers of `.com` driver images that no `retail_binaries.py` entry covers.
- **Open:** a tweak that redraws the lit part of the Raptor's reverse throttle bar on a full redraw, which retail's `ThrottleSlider_PaintH` leaves undrawn while the HERC backs up ([the bars](../es1/simulation/cockpit-throttle-gauge.md#the-bars)); the port draws it as retail does, the user's choice. The tweak's setting, its name and its localized strings are not built.
