# Plan — ES1's first playable mission

**The port of *Metaltech: Earthsiege*'s simulation and shell: the decisions made with the user, what the port still leaves out, and the steps.** It began with a first playable slice, a mission loaded in the ES1 zone viewer with the player's HERC under the player's control, on ES1's tick, against enemy HERCs that take damage and run ES1's AI. The slice and the modules after it are ported, but for the digital driver's mix, which is deferred ([the sound plan](plan-es1-sound.md#the-modules)). The work is port first: reverse-engineering is limited to the gaps a port step meets, read as a targeted batch before that step. This plan carries out [phase 2](plan-es1-support.md#phase-2--the-engine) of the ES1 plan for the simulation.

## Rules

- Behaviour is ported from what the binary shows. Nothing is invented to reach a playable build sooner; where the slice lacks a retail feature, the code does nothing in its place, tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` and listed under [what the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out) until its step lands.
- No shortcut that a later step has to undo: a structure retail builds (the weapon mounts, the view's window) is built as retail builds it, even when the step that uses it fully comes later.
- The ES1 docs describe ES1; the port's types and departures go in doc comments on the C#, citing the doc section.
- Each module is split into parts with the user before it starts and taken to [done](plan-es1-support.md#when-a-module-is-done) before the next: RE batches, each an [ES1 batch](handoff-es1-data-formats.md#how-the-batches-were-run) limited to what the next port part needs and written up in the ES1 docs before the port reads them, then the port parts, one doc batch over their details, and the user's check against retail where retail can be seen. A module's positive control is the retail check in the Open of the docs it wrote.

## What the slice leaves out

Until its step lands, each of these does nothing, tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` in code:

- **The digital driver's mix**, the sound plan's module E, deferred ([the sound plan](plan-es1-sound.md#module-e-the-digital-driver)): the effects, the pilots' voice lines, the songs' drums and the shell's click, talking heads' lines and `.TTM` sounds play through decision 26's stand-in for the digital driver.
- **The shell's loading screen**, no step yet: screen 9999 sets the exit code and leaves the screen as it was, where retail makes a `SimLoadMenu`, loads the palette `res32\regn9999.dpl`, plays `anm\esiege2.anm` and copies its rect to the visible page ([changing screens](../es1/shell/menus.md#changing-screens--shell_handlemessage-0003c7d8)).

## Decisions
Made with the user, each under the number the code cites it by:

1\. **The animation classes are the core's.** `ShapeAnimation`, `AnimationThread`, `ShapeInstance` and `SkeletonPose` are in `Herculan.Engine.Anim`, since the 3Space classes belong to the core ([how the two games share code](planning.md#how-the-two-games-share-code)). ES1's are ES2's but for one rounding: a seek scales the position with ES1's rounding Q14 multiply ([differences](../es1/rendering/dts-node-posing.md#differences-from-es2)). The caller of a seek names its arithmetic (`SeekRounding`), so neither game's behaviour changes and the core tests no game.

2\. **The tick is paced as retail paces it.** ES1 runs one tick a frame, its length the time the frame took (`SimTickDelta`, Q8 with 1.0 = 100 ms, held to 25–176 ms), and a frame waits at least two PC timer ticks ([the tick length](../es1/simulation/fixed-point-math.md#the-tick-length--time_beginsimtick-00063f40)). The timer runs at 120 interrupts a second ([the PC timer](../es1/simulation/timer.md#the-rate)), so retail's minimum frame time is two of them, 16.7 ms, which the tick's floor counts as 25 ms: a machine that draws a frame that fast plays at 1.5 times real time, where the hardware of its day took 25 ms or more a frame and played in real time. Every frame past a view change's cooldown also ends waiting for the monitor's next vertical retrace, 70 a second ([the view command](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#cockpitview_processviewcommand-000226f0)), a wait that ends before the next tick is due on a machine that keeps up, and so does not slow it. An ES1 pacer runs a tick once **three** interrupts' time, 25 ms, has passed, with the elapsed time counted in 120 Hz interrupts as the tick's length under retail's clamp and arithmetic, holds the frame's end to the next retrace of a 70 Hz monitor as retail does, and shows the display every host frame, as retail draws no frame between ticks. The three-interrupt wait is this engine's departure from retail's two, the user's choice, so the game plays in real time as it did on period hardware; it is tagged in code and listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

3\. **The view is view 0 as retail builds it.** The world is drawn in the window and with the projection of the chassis's `.VUE` record, clipped by its clip regions, under the canopy art from `db0\<chassis>` ([the view manager](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#the-view-manager), [the canopy art](../es1/simulation/cockpit-views.md#loading-and-freeing-the-canopy-art)), from the eye-point camera `Cam_AttachTo` attaches. A free camera, an engine aid with no retail counterpart, stands in only where the player's machine has no cockpit view.

4\. **The sensor model is ported** from [`sensor-model.md`](../es1/simulation/sensor-model.md): the AI's target choice reads the contacts and radar flags `Sim_DetectionTick` sets.

6\. **A named mission loads as a single mission, and the simulator runs on the install's `data\` files.** The shell's mode-0 load writes `data\script.dat`, `data\mission.var` and `data\player.mec` into the install, and the simulator reads whatever those files hold, as `DBSIM.EXE` does, so it also runs on a set retail's shell wrote. A career's missions load through the shell's mode 1 ([a career's next mission](../es1/shell/career.md#a-careers-next-mission)). `--theater` and `--world` override header words 0, 8 and 9, as engine aids.

7\. **The 320×200 screen is shown at 4:3**, as a CRT showed it, with the `.VUE` projection in the 320×200 screen's pixels.

8\. **A textured face is filled as retail fills it**, row by row with the texture interpolated in screen space, the walk the scene's clip mode 2 selects ([bitmap fills](../es1/rendering/polygon-shading.md#bitmap-fills-brush-modes-5-and-7), [the display drivers](../es1/rendering/polygon-shading.md#the-display-drivers)), the walk run in the fragment shader as the clouds' is. The perspective-correct mapping the core's renderer already does is the "Perspective-correct textures" tweak, off by default.

9\. **A modern controller enters at the calibrated-axis level.** Retail times the game port's axis lines in port reads, a count whose worth depends on the machine, and maps it through a calibration the player records ([the joystick library](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#normalising-an-axis)). The port cannot exist on modern hardware, so each of its four axes arrives as the value a calibrated stick gives, −128 to +128, and everything after it is retail's: the devices, their response, the class readers, the poll and the merge ([the joystick devices](../es1/simulation/joystick.md#the-joystick-devices)). The controller's roles are wired onto the port as its own wiring: stick X and Y on axes 0 and 1, the rudder on axis 2, the throttle on axis 3. So the calibration panel (`00049a7c` and its neighbours) and `data\joy1.cal` and `joy2.cal` are left out for good: the files are not read, and the controls panel's CALIBRATE rebuilds the devices and raises no panel. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

10\. **The stick model comes from preferences option 7**, as retail's: `data\prefs.cfg`'s, changed on the controls panel's JOYSTICK row, which rebuilds the devices when the panel closes ([the controls panel](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#the-controls-panel--controlspanel_ctor-00048408)).

11\. **A panel runs inside the tick, as retail's does.** Retail raises a panel from the tick's commands and runs the panel's own loop there until it closes, then finishes the tick with its answer ([the loop](../es1/simulation/alert-panels.md#the-loop), [`PreferencesPanel_Raise`](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#raising-the-panel--preferencespanel_raise-00057820)). The port suspends the tick where retail enters the loop and runs one pass of the loop a host frame, the PC timer running on, where retail's loop runs with no wait or, for the preferences panel, once a retrace; the tick goes on from there when the panel closes, as the frame's end goes on after a wait for the monitor (decision 2).

12\. **`Ctrl+Q`'s and `Ctrl+Esc`'s QUIT ends the mission**, which closes the viewer, as retail's quit flag ends the simulator; `Q` puts the mission's status up in the status panel, as retail's does ([commands](../es1/simulation/main-loop.md#commands--sim_dispatchcommand-00059df8)).

13\. **The preferences panel's DONE always saves options 0–6.** Retail's saves them only when one differs from its load-time copy, which the controls panel's DONE makes current, so a change made before CONTROLS is opened and closed is lost at the next mission ([saving](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#saving)). The user chose the fix; it is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

14\. **A stick the controls panel builds no device for is left with none**, as the code means to: choosing `NONE` leaves no stick enabled, and a model that needs one stick leaves the second slot empty. Retail's `ControlsPanel_ApplyDevices` enables the device it has just freed in each case ([rebuilding the joystick devices](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#rebuilding-the-joystick-devices--controlspanel_applydevices-00048e98), whose Open asks what that device then reads); the departure is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

15\. **Each program's sound manager is the one `resource.cfg` makes** (decision 25). With none, the shell's start-up and the mission's start reset options 0–2, and the preferences panel's MUSIC, SOUNDS and RADIO follow the simulator's manager (`Sound_Manager`, `000959ac`, [the preferences panel's loop](../es1/simulation/preferences.md#the-loop--preferencespanel_run-00047a88)).

16\. **A click on the gunsight picks the object drawn at the clicked pixel in this engine's draw.** Retail's pixel pick ([the pick](../es1/simulation/terrain.md#the-pixel-pick-and-the-occlusion-probe)) keeps the last object its farthest-first painter's draw paints over the point; this engine draws the shapes and HERCs with a depth test, so the pick reads back which object the GPU drew at the point, the one the player sees under the pointer. The two differ only where retail's sort paints a farther object over a nearer one. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md` with the depth test.

17\. **The MFD's NAV MAP and missile camera draw the world with the GPU, into the MFD's screen rect.** Retail draws the NAV MAP's zone shapes and the missile camera's scene with its software rasterizer, in palette indices, into the MFD's rect ([the NAV MAP's paint](../es1/simulation/mfd-nav-map.md#the-paint), [the missile camera](../es1/simulation/mfd-missile-camera.md)). This engine draws them as it draws view 0's world, in a world pass into the rect at the window's resolution, and marks as world only the cells the pass covers, so the NAV MAP's fill and cross, and whatever else the 2D drawing leaves there, stay palette indices shown through the live palette. The shapes are sharper than retail's and drawn with this engine's depth test (decision 16). The user's choice.

18\. **The world's colors follow the live palette.** Retail's whole screen is palette indices shown through the one live palette, so whatever is installed into it shows on the world as on the cockpit ([installing colors](../es1/rendering/palette.md#installing-colors)). This engine draws the world on the GPU in full color (decisions 8 and 17). The tables the shaded, textured and solid faces read hold palette indices, which the GPU shows through its own copy of the live palette, so a change to the live palette sends that copy its 256 colors and rebuilds no table of the faces'. The world's other palette-derived colors are rebuilt from the new palette: the sky's and the ground's bands, the ground's tables, the skyline, the clouds and the scattered points. The death flash and the hit flash ([the hit shake](../es1/simulation/cockpit-panel.md#the-hit-shake)) so reach the whole world as well as the cockpit art. A change reaches the screen at the frame's end, as retail's `Palette_FlushDirtyRange` sends it ([installing colors](../es1/rendering/palette.md#installing-colors)), and a world pass draws in the live palette as that send will show it, so a frame is shown in one palette. A world pixel keeps the colors it was drawn in until it is drawn again, where retail's display recolors every pixel at once: the main view takes a fade's step and the pitch gradient's entries one frame late, and a cockpit rect's world when it is next painted (listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`; a world layer of indices is [Open](#open)). The user's choice.

19\. **The Heads-Down Display's command screen draws its map's ground as decision 17's NAV MAP does.** Retail draws the zone's shapes seen from above with its software rasterizer, every lit face in palette entry `0x38` through ramp 14 ([drawing the map](../es1/simulation/hdd-command-screen.md#drawing-the-map)); this engine draws them in a world pass into the map viewport with that ramp pointed at the one entry, and the grid, the border, the markers and the chosen unit's frame stay palette indices drawn over it. The user's choice.

20\. **A tape plays at the pace it was recorded.** Retail's playback skips `Time_BeginSimTick` and gives each frame the tape's recorded `SimTickDelta` ([recording and playback](../es1/simulation/input.md#recording-and-playback)), so its frames come as fast as the machine draws them, which on a modern machine is the 70 Hz retrace the frame's end waits for (decision 2), where the retail tapes were recorded at 25 to 176 ms a frame. The port holds each playback frame for its recorded delta, as ES2's `--play` does ([playback](input-tapes.md#playback)), so a demo plays in the time it was recorded in, as live play does under decision 2. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

22\. **The shell's typed characters are the host's typed text.** Retail's shell lets the PC BIOS have every key its keyboard subscribers do not claim, and reads the characters the BIOS's own US table makes of them through DOS ([the input devices](../es1/simulation/input.md#the-input-devices)), code that is not in `GO.EXE`. This engine stands a key buffer in for the BIOS's, each key's character the one the host types for it, so the REGISTRATION window's name box takes letters as the player's keyboard layout gives them. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

24\. **A pass of the lift's ride takes three PC timer interrupts' drawing time.** Each pass of `LiftStart_Rise` ends in `Vga_WaitVerticalRetrace`, which ends at once while a retrace is in progress ([the ride](../es1/simulation/lift-start.md#the-ride--liftstart_rise-00057ffc)), so on retail's machines a pass took as long as it took to draw, rounded up to a retrace. The port draws in no time, so each pass stands 25 ms, decision 2's frame minimum, before its frame's end, and the 115 passes take about 3.3 s. The user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

25\. **The sound devices are the install's `resource.cfg`'s**, as retail's setup writes them ([the driver](../es1/sound/sound-drivers.md#the-driver)): a `[SoundFX]` `Driver#` turns the digital sounds on, and a `[Music]` `Driver#` plays the songs through the FM driver, routed as retail routes a song's tracks for `A002` ([the devices](../es1/sound/song-player.md#the-devices)); a blank `Driver#` makes no sound manager, as retail's does. Only the sound the music was written for is ported: the FM driver and device `0xa005`. A `Driver#` naming another MIDI device (Sound Master II, MPU 401, MT-32, AWE 32) plays FM too, where retail's would route the songs to that device; the departure is listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`, and those devices are [Open](plan-es1-sound.md#open). The user's choice.

26\. **A stand-in plays the digital sounds in place of HMI's digital driver, whose port (the sound plan's module E) is deferred**: the gain is the start block's volume over `0x7fff`, linear as the driver's mixer is ([module E](plan-es1-sound.md#module-e-the-digital-driver)); the pan is the center, as retail's sounds play on the user's mono `e015` device; and the host's mixer resamples the 11,025 Hz data and mixes it in floating point, where retail's mixer adds 8-bit samples and clips them. Each departure is tagged `// PLACEHOLDER:` in code. The user's choice.

29\. **Placed sounds play in stereo, panned across the camera's view.** Retail's placement pans a sound by its offset along the world's x axis, whatever the camera's heading, rounds a sound within 1,024 units of the camera to a whole axis, and reaches only a voice as it starts ([placed sounds](../es1/sound/sound-manager.md#placed-sounds)); on the user's mono `e015` device every sound plays in the center (decision 26). The "Stereo sound" tweak, on by default, pans each placed sound by where it lies across the camera's view, every frame and on a voice already playing; off, every sound plays in the center. Retail's placement still sets the volume either way. This engine's invention, the user's choice; listed in `KNOWN_ISSUES_ES1.md`.

30\. **The core's "High resolution widescreen" tweak, on by default, draws the mission at the window's resolution and shape.** Off, the world is drawn in the 320×200 screen's own pixels, as retail rasterizes it, and the screen is shown at 4:3 with bars at its sides (decision 7). On, every world pass is drawn at the window's resolution, decision 17's and 19's included, and a window wider than 4:3 shows more of the canvas at the cockpit's sides. The user's choice.

46\. **The minimum and medium installs are supported, not only the full one.** The disc's installer offers all three sizes ([the installer's `ES.BAT`](../es1/shell/launcher.md#the-installers-esbat)), and the install window offers them as its size choice, the full one first chosen; `--install` stays Earthsiege 2's. An install is Metaltech: Earthsiege's when its root holds `GO.EXE` or `RESOURCE.CFG`, which every size copies from `STUFF` and no Earthsiege 2 install holds; `vol\simsound.vol`, the expansion installer's test, is in every Earthsiege 2 install too. Its layout is its own `ES.BAT`'s: one that sets `PREFER_ALT` has the install for `ALT_DRIVE` and the disc for the CD's current folder, where the programs, the flics and the archives left on the CD are, and the shell's and simulator's archives are the install's and then the disc's ([the volume group](../es1/formats/streams.md#the-volume-group)). Such an install started with no disc kept asks for one before it runs, where retail's `ES.BAT` fails changing to the CD drive. Every size writes `data\addr.bat`, as retail's script does. The user's choice.

## Open

- **Open:** the calibration panel's code, `cal_alrt.cpp`, which decision 9 leaves out of the port, read with the library region ([`handoff-es1-library-region.md`](handoff-es1-library-region.md)): the unreferenced cluster `0004571c` before `alert.cpp` and the four small functions among the `Prefs_*` helpers.
- **Open:** every placeholder [the slice leaves out](#what-the-slice-leaves-out), each an **Unported:** item in `ROADMAP.md` once its step lands.
- **Open:** a world layer of palette indices that the screen resolves through the live palette as it does its cells, so a palette change recolors every world pixel at once (decision 18). Every face path of the scene program has its palette index before it looks up a color, and the billboards are indices already; the backdrop's programs, the sky's and the ground's bands, the skyline, the clouds, the scattered points and the textured ground's fade tables, resolve colors themselves. Two colors are no index: the shade table's mid grey for a cell naming no ramp, and an entry the palette lacks. The scene program is the core's, shared with ES2's full-color world. Before the target's format changes, check what `IndexedWorldSurface.ReadWorldCoverage` and `Es1WorldPick` read back from it.
- **Open:** a tweak that redraws the lit part of the Raptor's reverse throttle bar on a full redraw, which retail's `ThrottleSlider_PaintH` leaves undrawn while the HERC backs up ([the bars](../es1/simulation/cockpit-throttle-gauge.md#the-bars)); the port draws it as retail does, the user's choice. The tweak's setting, its name and its localized strings are not built.
